# Fair Housing Advertising

**In short:** Federal law (Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. 3604(c)) makes it illegal to publish any ad for housing that shows a preference or limitation based on a protected class. That covers listing remarks, social posts, flyers, photos, and who you target with paid ads. The easy rule: describe the property, not the people who might live there. Many states and cities add more protected classes than the federal list, so you need to know your local list too.

## What the law says
- Section 3604(c) bars making, printing or publishing any notice, statement or advertisement for the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on a protected class. HUD's rule on this is 24 C.F.R. 100.75 (both cited in the HUD v. Facebook charge, from search summary). The regulations are in 24 CFR Part 100: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/subtitle-B/chapter-I/part-100
- The ad does not need to be intentional. What matters is how an ordinary reader would take it.
- Liability can reach the agent, the brokerage, and the platform or vendor involved.

## Protected classes
**Federal (7):** race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status (families with children under 18, pregnant people), disability (HUD's regs say "handicap").

**Common state/local additions (varies a lot, check yours):** source of income, marital status, age, military/veteran status, citizenship or immigration status, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, and others. Which of these exist depends on your state or city. (I did not build a 50-state table here. Check your state fair housing agency's list.)

## Describe the property, not the people
- Say what the home has (3 bedrooms, step-free entry, fenced yard), not who it is "perfect for".
- Avoid naming or hinting at the type of buyer, neighborhood demographics, churches, schools by "quality" or "type of people".
- Do not describe a school or neighborhood as "good", "safe", "family-friendly". Link to neutral public data and let buyers decide.
- Accessibility features can be described factually ("roll-in shower", "first-floor primary suite"). Do not say "not suitable for wheelchairs".
- Photos and video count as ads too. Showing only one type of person in your marketing can be risky. The Fair Housing logo/statement in ads is common practice, but it does not make a discriminatory ad legal.
- NAR points out there is no authoritative official list of "banned words". Context decides (NAR presentation, from search summary): https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/documents/slides-from-window-to-the-law-how-to-avoid-illegal-marketing.pdf

## Risky phrases and safer rewrites
These are examples of phrases that regulators, fair housing groups and trainers commonly flag. They are not an official HUD list.

| Risky phrase | Why it is risky | Safer rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "Perfect for young families" | Familial status / age | "Three bedrooms and a fenced backyard" |
| "Great for singles" or "bachelor pad" | Familial status, marital status, sex | "Open-plan one-bedroom, 10 minutes to downtown" |
| "Adult community" / "no kids" | Familial status (unless a legal 55+/62+ community) | State only what the legal HOA/community rules are, with the legal basis |
| "Walking distance to church" | Religion | "0.3 miles to Main St. shops and bus stop" |
| "Master bedroom" | Some groups flag it (discussion only; not a federal rule) | "Primary bedroom" (often MLS-preferred) |
| "Exclusive neighborhood" | Can imply exclusion | "Gated community, 24-hour guard" (if factual) |
| "Safe neighborhood" | Steering by demographics | "Alarm system installed; see local police crime map" (link to neutral data) |
| "Great school district" | Steering toward or away from groups | "Address assigned to X Elementary (see district website)" |
| "Able-bodied", "walk-up only for fit buyers" | Disability | "Third-floor unit, 24 steps, no elevator" |
| "Mother-in-law suite" | Familial status/sex framing | "Detached guest suite with kitchenette" |
| "No Section 8" / "no vouchers" | Source of income (illegal in many places) | Leave out; follow the local law |
| "English speakers only" | National origin | Omit |
| "Ideal for retirees" or "empty nesters" | Age / familial status | "Single-level living, low-maintenance yard" |

## Digital ads and targeting
- Paid social and display ads for housing must not exclude people by protected class. The big example is Meta: the DOJ settlement (2022) required Meta to stop its "Special Ad Audience" tool for housing ads and to build a Variance Reduction System; DOJ posted an update on Feb 6, 2025 (from search summary). Sources: https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/justice-department-and-meta-platforms-inc-reach-key-agreement-they-implement-groundbreaking and https://www.nar.realtor/legal-case-summaries/meta-facebook-settles-fair-housing-violation-allegations
- Use the platform's "housing/special ad category" option and do not try to work around it.

## Enforcement news and status (2023-2026)
- **HUD v. Facebook** (charge filed 2019, platform case, background): https://archives.hud.gov/news/2019/pr19-035.cfm
- **Zillow and New Jersey (April 2023):** Zillow signed a collaboration agreement with the NJ Attorney General's civil rights division on product features to help prevent discriminatory ads, after earlier work on discriminatory rental listing language: https://www.nj.gov/oag/newsreleases23/2023-0418_Zillow-NJOAG-Collaboration-Agreement-Executed.pdf
- **DOJ and Meta (2022-2025):** see above.
- **HUD pulled back its 2024 digital-ad guidance.** HUD's April 29, 2024 guidance on advertising through digital platforms was among eight documents HUD withdrew. The Federal Register notice (published April 6, 2026) says the withdrawal took effect September 17, 2025: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/06/2026-06624/notification-of-withdrawal-of-fair-housing-and-equal-opportunity-guidance-documents . HUD said conduct that violates the text of the Fair Housing Act is still enforceable. The statute did not change, and state and local laws still apply. Commentary: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/hud-withdraws-eight-fair-housing-8645237/
- **Older HUD advertising memo:** The 1995 Achtenberg memo (HUD Jan 9, 1995) is at https://hud.gov/sites/documents/DOC_7784.PDF . An April 2026 Oregon Realtors article says HUD removed it without notice and that it is "technically valid" but not something HUD asks people to rely on (secondary source, verify): https://oregonrealtors.org/news/tip-of-the-week/fair-housing-compliance-after-the-guidance-reset/
- **Federal enforcement capacity:** reports in 2025-2026 describe HUD fair housing staff and grant cuts, with court fights over grants to local fair housing groups (advocacy and press sources, e.g. https://themortgagepoint.com/2025/07/25/the-state-of-fair-housing/ and https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2025/09/huds-new-direction-in-fair-housing-act-enforcement-and-rescission-of-certain-office-of-fair-housing-and-equal-opportunity-guidance/). Practical takeaway: less federal enforcement does not mean less risk. State agencies, local fair housing centers and private lawsuits remain active, and you stay liable under the statute.
- **Gap:** I did not find a verified 2024-2026 HUD charge against an individual agent's listing ad. (unverified — check HUD and your state agency press pages for current examples before citing any.)

## What to do
- [ ] Read every ad (remarks, captions, hashtags, alt text) for people-based words before posting.
- [ ] Replace "who it is for" language with features and facts.
- [ ] Link to neutral data for schools and crime instead of judging neighborhoods.
- [ ] Use the platform's housing ad category for paid social. Do not target by age, sex, ZIP-as-proxy for race, etc.
- [ ] Look up your state and city protected classes (source of income, marital status, age, military status, gender identity).
- [ ] Have your broker review team or brokerage ad templates.
- [ ] Re-check any AI-written copy before publishing. See [[AI in Real Estate - Rules & Risks]].
- [ ] Keep records of ads you published, in case of a complaint.
