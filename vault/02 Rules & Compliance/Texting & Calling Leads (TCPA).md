# Texting & Calling Leads (TCPA)

**In short:** The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) and the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule limit how you can call and text people for marketing. The safest habits: only contact people who gave you their number and agreed to hear from you, reply to people who contact you first, honor "stop" immediately, check the Do Not Call list, and stay within calling hours. Cold-texting a bought list is the highest-risk move, and TCPA lawsuits can mean statutory damages per message. (Damages amounts: $500 per violation, up to $1,500 if willful, is the commonly cited range. I did not verify it from a source in this session: check before citing.)

## The "one-to-one consent" rule: dead (verify nothing replaced it)
- The FCC adopted a rule in 2023 that would have required consent to be given to one seller at a time ("one-to-one") and tied to the topic of the site where the person consented. It was set to start January 27, 2025.
- On January 24, 2025 the 11th Circuit, in *Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC* (No. 24-10277), vacated that part of the rule, holding the FCC went beyond the TCPA's "prior express consent" language and relying on *Loper Bright*. Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/advertising-marketing-branding/1577820/11th-circuit-strikes-down-fccs-one-to-one-consent-rule , https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/alerts-otherindustries-eleventh-circuit-deals-fatal-blow , https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/one-to-none-the-fcc-and-eleventh-circuit-issue-last-minute-rulings-gutting-the-one-to-one-tcpa-consent-rule.html , https://www.kaufmandolowich.com/news-resources/eleventh-circuit-vacates-the-federal-communications-commissions-one-to-one-consent-rule-by-richard-perr-monica-littman-dominic-borelli-tabitha-mangano-and-kristen-ruotolo-3-5-2025/
- Practical meaning: lead-generation consent forms that name many "partners" are not barred by that rule. They can still be challenged under other law, and your lead vendor's consent language still matters. Whether the FCC has since tried a new rule: (unverified — check fcc.gov).

## Courts need not follow FCC interpretations
In *McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson* (June 20, 2025), the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that district courts are not bound by the FCC's TCPA interpretations. Commentators expect more defenses based on this. Sources: https://natlawreview.com/node/313385/printable/print , https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102kq4q/supreme-court-gives-courts-wide-latitude-to-disregard-fcc-guidance-on-the-telepho/ . The effect on your daily practice: rules can differ by court. Stay conservative.

## Revoking consent ("stop" means stop)
- An FCC rule on revoking consent was delayed several times. Per law-firm summaries, the "revoke-all" part was pushed to January 31, 2027, and the FCC later issued a revised framework. One firm says an effective date of November 25, 2026 applies to the revised rules; others say 30 days after Federal Register publication. The sources conflict (unverified — check the Federal Register/FCC): https://www.burr.com/telephone-consumer-protection-act/the-fcc-delays-effective-date-of-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-until-january-31-2027 , https://www.bhfs.com/insight/new-tcpa-revocation-of-consent-rules-take-effect-on-november-25/ , https://www.troutman.com/insights/fcc-revises-tcpa-revocation-of-consent-rules-that-were-set-to-go-into-effect-in-january/ , https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-312A1.pdf
- Whatever the rule date, treat any reasonable "stop", "unsubscribe", "don't text me" as an opt-out right away and log it.

## Calling hours and Do Not Call
- Telemarketing calls are barred before 8 a.m. and after 9 p.m. in the person's local time (FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule summary; secondary sources say it is the recipient's time zone, verify): https://www.ftc.gov/advertising-and-marketing/telemarketing and https://consumer.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/telemarketing
- Some states are stricter. Example: Florida's telephone solicitation law (as amended 2023 by HB 761) reportedly uses 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. and has a carve-out for licensees calling sellers who put out their own sign or ad (from search summary, verify in § 501.059): https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/501.059 and https://laws.flrules.org/2023/150
- National Do Not Call Registry: personal numbers including cell phones; business and fax numbers not covered. Sellers are supposed to scrub lists against the registry regularly (a New York guide says every 31 days): https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/donotcall.pdf and https://telemarketing.donotcall.gov/faq/faqbusinessforsplashpage.aspx
- Exceptions (established business relationship, e.g., an inquiry or transaction in the recent past) exist, but the time limits and rules are specific. The "18 months" figure is often quoted by vendors; I could not confirm it: (unverified — check ftc.gov).
- Expired listings and FSBO calls: whether these are exempt depends on the registry, your state, and whether the call is a solicitation. (unverified — ask your broker's compliance person. Many brokerages require a DNC scrub for these.)

## A2P 10DLC: carrier registration for texting tools
- Business texts sent by software (CRM, bulk, automated) through a normal 10-digit number must be registered with carriers (brand + campaign). Unregistered traffic gets blocked or filtered. Sources disagree on the exact cutoff date (2023 to early 2025 depending on carrier/provider), so ask your texting vendor: https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration , https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/14910496447771-Shutdown-of-Unregistered-10DLC-Messaging , https://telnyx.com/resources/what-is-10dlc.md
- CTIA's messaging principles (an industry code, not law) expect clear opt-in, STOP/HELP handling: https://telnyx.com/resources/ctia-sms-guidelines (vendor summary).
- Registration is about carrier delivery. It does not give you TCPA consent.

## What's usually OK vs risky
| Situation | Typical risk |
|---|---|
| Replying to a text/call/form from someone who contacted you about a property | Lower. Answer what they asked; don't pile on promos. |
| Texting a past client who gave you their number and agreed to updates | Lower, if they never opted out and you can prove consent. |
| Lead from your own website form with clear "I agree to be contacted by [your name] by call/text" | Lower. Keep a record (screenshot/time stamp). |
| Purchased list / lead vendor with vague "partners" consent | Higher. Ask the vendor for proof of consent per lead. |
| Cold texting numbers from a list or scraped data | High. Avoid. |
| Autodialing/robocalls or pre-recorded messages without consent | High. Avoid. |
| Contacting after someone says "stop" | High. Stop and log it. |
| Calling expired/FSBO leads | Depends. Scrub DNC, follow state rules, keep to hours. |

The Supreme Court's *Facebook v. Duguid* narrowed the federal "autodialer" definition (from search summary; verify), but state "mini-TCPA" laws (Florida and others) and plain consent requirements still make cold texting dangerous.

## What to do
- [ ] Put clear consent language on every form: who will contact them, by call/text, and that they can say STOP.
- [ ] Save proof of consent (date, page, wording, phone number).
- [ ] Add "Reply STOP to opt out" to marketing texts and honor it immediately.
- [ ] Scrub call lists against the Do Not Call registry; keep your own internal DNC list.
- [ ] Call/text between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. recipient time to be safe (stricter than 9 p.m. federal limit, and matches stricter states).
- [ ] Register your texting tool for A2P 10DLC (ask the vendor).
- [ ] Ask your lead vendor for per-lead consent records before texting.
- [ ] Use scripts from [[Lead Follow-Up Scripts]]. For email, see [[Email Marketing Rules (CAN-SPAM)]].
- [ ] Tell your broker about any complaint or demand letter immediately.
