# AI in Real Estate: Rules & Risks

**In short:** You can use AI to draft listing copy and edit photos, but you are the one responsible for what gets published. Three risks matter most: (1) altered or virtually staged photos must be disclosed in many MLSs and, in California since January 1, 2026, by law; (2) AI can write fair-housing-violating copy; (3) AI output may not be copyrightable, and it may be wrong. Always review, fact-check, and label.

## Altered and virtually staged photos
- **California AB 723 (effective January 1, 2026).** Applies to licensees who digitally alter listing images (Business and Professions Code 10140.8, per search results). Core idea: disclose the alteration and make the unaltered original available, including a link/URL/QR where the ad is online. The law regulates disclosure; it does not ban virtual staging. - **MLS rules vary.** Examples reported in secondary sources: CRMLS requires disclosure of all digitally altered images (Rule 11.5.2, cited by a vendor page); Canopy MLS wants disclosure on the image; Bright MLS wants it in caption and remarks; Doorify MLS does not require labeling (photographer/vendor blogs, verify in each MLS rulebook): https://www.styldod.com/blog/mls-ai-photo-compliance-rules-2026 , https://georgesheldon.com/why-pennsylvania-and-maryland-agents-should-avoid-using-ai-to-alter-listing-photos/ . Read your own MLS rules.
- **NAR ethics.** Article 12 requires presenting a "true picture" in advertising. NAR has an article warning that AI-enhanced listing photos can be legally risky: https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/law-and-ethics/using-ai-to-enhance-listing-photos-can-be-legally-risky . Its line: improving the buyer's understanding versus misleading them. Cited provision "SOP 12-5" on disclosing altered photos comes from a secondary source (verify in the Code of Ethics).
- **Practical line.** Fine, if labeled: virtual staging that adds furniture. Risky: removing power lines, adding a view, repairing visible damage, changing finishes, or widening rooms. Never use AI to hide defects.

## Fair Housing risks of AI-written text
- AI tools can produce phrases like "perfect for young families", "quiet, safe area" or "walk to church". These can violate the Fair Housing Act, and you published them. See [[Fair Housing Advertising]].
- HUD's 2024 guidance on AI/digital ad targeting was withdrawn (notice April 6, 2026, effective Sept 17, 2025), but HUD said conduct violating the Act is still enforceable: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/06/2026-06624/notification-of-withdrawal-of-fair-housing-and-equal-opportunity-guidance-documents . State and local laws are not withdrawn.
- HousingWire checklist before pasting AI text into the MLS: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ai-fair-housing-remarks-checklist/
- Don't let AI tools target ads by protected traits or use zip codes as proxies.

## Accuracy and disclosure
- AI can invent features (a pool, new roof, school names, square footage). You must check each fact against the MLS data, tax records, or the seller. NAR's guidance says AI output can be inaccurate and must still meet duties under Articles 2 and 12 (via a secondary summary: https://aiandrealtors.com/ai-compliance , verify the NAR text).
- Don't paste client names, offers or private details into public AI tools. Check your brokerage's AI policy.

## Copyright of AI text and images
- The U.S. Copyright Office's January 29, 2025 report (Part 2, copyrightability) says prompts alone are generally not enough for human authorship; protection depends on human contributions (selection, arrangement, editing), judged case by case. Firm summaries: https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/copyright-office-publishes-report , https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-generative-ai , https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2025/01/copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-content (the report itself was not read here).
- What it means for you: raw AI text or images may be unprotectable, so a competitor could copy them. If you rewrite and add real local knowledge, your human edits are what carry protection.
- MLS photos and descriptions have separate license terms from your MLS. Don't use another agent's photos or copy another listing's remarks.

## What to do
- [ ] Label any virtually staged or AI-edited photo as your MLS and state require. In California, also provide access to the original image.
- [ ] Never alter material features or conditions of the property.
- [ ] Run every AI draft through a fair housing check ([[Fair Housing Advertising]]) and a fact check against the MLS record.
- [ ] Keep original photos and the AI prompts/drafts for your records.
- [ ] Edit AI copy so it reflects real knowledge: [[Listing Description Guide]].
- [ ] Don't enter personal client data into public AI tools.
- [ ] Ask your broker for the brokerage AI policy.
