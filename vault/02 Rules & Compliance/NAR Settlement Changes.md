# NAR Settlement Changes (2024 onward)

**In short:** Since August 17, 2024, agents who work with buyers through an MLS must have a written agreement with the buyer before touring a home, and offers of compensation to buyer brokers can no longer be posted on a Realtor-affiliated MLS. Commissions are negotiable and are not set by law. Lawsuits and some state laws are still moving, so check your state and your broker's forms.

## Timeline of the case
- **March 15, 2024:** NAR agreed to settle the Sitzer/Burnett commission class action for $418 million (from search summary): https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-settles-commission-lawsuits-for-418-million/
- **Aug 17, 2024:** practice changes took effect.
- **Nov 26, 2024:** Judge Stephen R. Bough (W.D. Missouri) granted final approval (reported by Inman, RISMedia, HousingWire, NAR Magazine): https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/judge-approves-nar-settlement-in-sitzer-burnett-case
- **Nov 2024:** the DOJ filed a statement saying the settlement should not shield NAR from future government enforcement. NAR called the filing non-binding: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-doj-commission-lawsuit-settlement-agreement/
- **Dec 2024:** an objector (Spring Way Center, with several home sellers) appealed the approval (from search summary): https://www.inman.com/2024/12/02/landmark-nar-commission-settlement-faces-first-appeal/ . The appeal's outcome: (unverified — check before relying on this).
- **Jan 2025:** final judgment filed in Sitzer/Burnett: https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/01/31/final-judgment-puts-a-bow-on-nar-commissions-deal

## The practice changes (for agents on Realtor MLSs)
1. **Written agreement before touring.** If you are an MLS participant working with a buyer, you need a written agreement with that buyer before they tour a home (whether virtual tours count: unverified, ask your broker). NAR's agent-facing explainer: https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/homebuyers-what-the-nar-settlement-means and the reminder: https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/national-association-of-realtors-reminds-members-and-consumers-of-real-estate-practice-change
2. **The agreement doesn't have to be exclusive representation.** Search results (from NAR-based summaries) say a non-representation agreement, including one for a single property, can satisfy the rule. Details of what must be in it come from your MLS and broker form.
3. **Compensation must be specific.** The agreement must state the compensation (amount or rate) clearly, not as an open-ended number (the settlement's exact wording: unverified, check NAR's text). The agent cannot receive more than the amount agreed in the buyer agreement. (From summary: https://rworld.com/blog/written-agreements . Confirm exact wording with NAR's text.)
4. **No offers of compensation on the MLS.** Sellers can still offer to pay a buyer's agent, but not through listing fields on the MLS. Those offers happen off-MLS (in writing between parties, in the contract, etc.). Source: https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/sales-marketing/6-ways-the-relationship-between-real-estate-agents-and-consumers-has-improved
5. **Disclosures.** Sources agree commission rates are negotiable and not set by law (HousingWire pieces below). The exact disclosure wording your forms must use: confirm with your broker.
6. **Residential only.** The changes cover residential deals, not commercial or leases (NAR summary above).
7. **Existing agreements.** If your broker has older buyer agreements, they must be updated to include the required terms.

## State laws are diverging
Some states changed or added their own buyer-agreement rules in 2025. Examples from HousingWire reporting (verify against each state's statute):
- Mississippi and Alabama passed laws that let buyers elect brokerage services by signing an agreement, but did not make an agreement mandatory before showings.
- Texas lawmakers in 2025 required a written agreement before a licensee takes substantive action for a buyer, but Texas does not require it before a showing.
- Oregon enacted a requirement for buyer representation agreements before or shortly after an agent assists a buyer.
Sources: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/states-override-nar-buyer-agreement/ and https://www.housingwire.com/articles/states-buyer-agency-rules/ . Exact statute numbers and effective dates: (unverified — check before relying on this).

## What to do
- [ ] Use your broker's current buyer agreement before touring any home with a buyer.
- [ ] State compensation clearly and make sure it can't change upward.
- [ ] Explain, in writing and out loud, that commissions are negotiable.
- [ ] Do not put compensation offers into MLS fields. Ask the listing agent how seller concessions are handled.
- [ ] Check your own state's statute: you may follow stricter or different rules than NAR's.
