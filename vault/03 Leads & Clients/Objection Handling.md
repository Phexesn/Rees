# Objection Handling

**In short:** An objection is usually a question or a worry in disguise. Listen, say back what you heard, answer honestly, and ask a gentle next question. Never mislead a client to win a deal, and never claim something about commissions or rules that is not true. All wording below is **original example language**.

## A simple 4-step pattern
1. **Listen** without interrupting.
2. **Acknowledge:** "That makes sense."
3. **Answer honestly**, including "I don't know, let me find out."
4. **Ask:** "Does that help? What else would you want to know?"

## Buyer objections

### "Is the price negotiable?"
**Say:** "Almost always, yes, in the sense that the seller can accept less than the asking price. How much room there is depends on the home, how long it has been listed, how many other buyers are interested and the seller's situation. I'll look at recent sales and the days on market, and we'll decide on an offer together."
**Do not say:** "They'll definitely take 10% less." You cannot know that.

### "I want to wait for rates to drop."
**Say:** "That's a reasonable thing to think about. Nobody can predict where rates will go, and I can't either. What I can do is show you what a payment looks like at today's rate, and what it would look like if rates were lower. Some buyers refinance later if rates fall; some find prices rise if more buyers come back into the market. Neither is guaranteed. A lender can run these numbers for you. Would you like me to introduce you to two?"
**Do not say:** "Rates are going up, buy now." Do not promise a refinance.

### "We're just looking."
**Say:** "Totally fine. That's how most people start. If it helps, I can send you homes that match what you like and you tell me what to stop sending. When it makes sense to talk to a lender or tour, I'm here. What are you hoping to see more of?"
**Why:** Pressure sends people to the next agent. Being helpful and low-key earns the next call. See [[Follow-Up Cadence]].

### "I'm already talking to another agent."
**Say:** "Good to hear you have help. If you have signed an agreement, please honor it. I wouldn't want to get in the way of that. If you haven't, you're free to choose who to work with. Either way, if you have a question, feel free to ask."
**Compliance note:** Don't induce a client to breach a written agreement with another broker. Ask your broker and review the NAR Code of Ethics duties on this.

### "Why should I sign a buyer agreement?"
**Background (cited):** Under the 2024 NAR settlement's practice changes, effective **August 17, 2024**, MLS participants working with buyers must have a written agreement with the buyer **before the buyer tours a home**, in person or virtually. NAR's consumer materials and state association FAQs say the agreement must state the compensation the agent will receive, and the agent may not receive more than the amount agreed in the buyer agreement. Compensation is negotiable and not set by law. Offers of compensation to buyer brokers can no longer be displayed on the MLS. Sources: [NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-written-buyer-agreements), [NAR notice](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/national-association-of-realtors-reminds-members-and-consumers-of-real-estate-practice-change), [Oregon Realtors FAQ](https://oregonrealtors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/NAR-Settlement-FAQ_4.24.pdf), and the agent-facing [NAR Q&A summary](https://rworld.com/blog/written-agreements) (from search summaries). Some states already required written buyer agreements before the settlement. See also [[NAR Settlement Changes]].

**Say:** "The agreement puts in writing what I'll do for you and how I'm paid, so there are no surprises. It's required by the rules my industry now follows before we tour homes together. The terms are negotiable. Let's go through it line by line. You can take it home or have a lawyer read it. It does not need to be a long commitment; we can discuss the length and cancellation terms."
**Do not say:** "The law requires you to sign my agreement." The requirement is on the agent and varies by state and form; and no national standard form exists. The US Department of Justice has raised concerns that the written-agreement-before-touring requirement may itself raise antitrust issues ([Scotsman Guide](https://www.scotsmanguide.com/news/doj-expresses-concerns-about-nar-buyer-agreements/); [RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2024/12/02/court-report-nar-settlement-approved-but-doj-will-not-back-down/)); check current rules with your broker and association.
**Do not say:** "Commission is always 3%" or "it's standard." Commission is negotiable.

### "Can't the seller just pay your commission?"
**Say:** "Sometimes a seller agrees to pay or contribute, and sometimes buyers pay part or all themselves. We write the amount in our agreement. If a seller pays something, it reduces what you owe me; I cannot be paid more than what we've agreed in writing."
**Note:** Verify your state's form and your broker's policy. See [[Commission & Net Sheet Basics]].

### "We can see homes on our own online. Why do we need you?"
**Say:** "You absolutely can and I'd encourage it. Where I help is in the parts that aren't online: whether the price fits recent sales, how to write an offer that protects you, what to ask the inspector, and managing deadlines. Let's see if it's worth it to you."

## Seller objections

### "Another agent offered a lower commission."
**Say:** "That's fair to compare. Commission is negotiable and fees do vary. I'd like to compare what's included: pricing strategy, marketing, photos, negotiation, how I handle showings and the offer on the buyer's side. Can you share what they offered to do for that fee? Then I'll show you my plan and net sheet so you can compare what you'd take home."
**Do not say:** "Cheaper agents do worse work." You can't know that. Do not disparage competitors.
**Data point:** Redfin reported average buyer's-agent commission around 2.4% in 2025 (Q3 2025: 2.42%), and that about 37% of recent sellers in a Redfin-commissioned survey negotiated or tried to negotiate their agent's commission ([Redfin Q3 2025 release](https://www.redfin.com/news/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Press-Release_Q3-commissions.pdf); [MortgagePoint](https://themortgagepoint.com/2025/12/31/buyers-agent-commissions-inch-up-as-slower-market-shifts-leverage-to-buyers/); from search summaries). Those are averages and exclude some transactions; they do not set a rate for you.

### "Our neighbor's house sold for more. Why can't we list higher?"
**Say:** "Let's look at that house together: size, condition, updates, timing. We'll adjust for the differences and compare the sale to others. If we list above the data, we risk sitting while newer listings take the attention. If you want to test a higher price, we can set a review date and a plan for adjusting." See [[CMA Basics]].

### "I want to wait for spring (or until the market improves)."
**Say:** "That's a valid choice. Let's compare: what would the extra carrying costs be (mortgage, taxes, insurance, utilities) against the chance of a better price? I can't promise next season will be better. I can show you last year's activity in our area."

### "Will you cut the commission if I list at your price?"
**Say:** "I'll tell you plainly what I charge and what's included. Let's talk about what matters to you, whether it's net proceeds, timing or service."

## Things never to say
- Guarantees: "You'll get $X," "It will sell in a week."
- Anything about the neighborhood's demographics, schools quality "for families" or "safe areas." See [[Fair Housing Advertising]].
- Disparaging remarks about other agents, sellers or buyers.
