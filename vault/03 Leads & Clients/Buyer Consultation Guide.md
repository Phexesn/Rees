# Buyer Consultation Guide

**In short:** The first buyer meeting is where you explain how you work, how you are paid, and what the process looks like, and where you learn what the buyer needs. Since August 17, 2024, you generally need a **written agreement with the buyer before touring homes** with them. Your job is to make that conversation clear and calm.

## Agenda (30-60 minutes)
1. Welcome and rapport (5 min)
2. How buying works and what you do (10 min)
3. The written buyer agreement and compensation (10-15 min)
4. Financing and pre-approval (10 min)
5. Needs, wants, timeline (10-15 min)
6. Next steps and communication (5 min)

Use [[Template - Buyer Intake Form]] during the meeting.

## 1. Rapport and expectations
- Ask how they got here and what they have already done.
- Ask how they prefer to communicate (call, text, email, WhatsApp, etc.) and their best hours.
- Explain how you will work: search setup, showings, offers, deadlines.

## 2. Written buyer agreements (cited)
- **What changed:** Under the NAR settlement practice changes effective **August 17, 2024**, MLS participants working with buyers must enter a written agreement with the buyer before the buyer tours a home, in person or virtually. The practice changes do not dictate a particular type of relationship; any legal written agreement that meets the requirements works. There is no national standard form. Sources: [NAR notice](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/national-association-of-realtors-reminds-members-and-consumers-of-real-estate-practice-change), [NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-written-buyer-agreements), [Oregon Realtors FAQ](https://oregonrealtors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/NAR-Settlement-FAQ_4.24.pdf), [HUD committee report (PDF, search result)](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/Housing/images/HCFAC%20FINAL%20%20APPROVED%20SUBCOMMITTEE%20REPORT%209.11.2024.pdf). See [[NAR Settlement Changes]].
- **Compensation terms:** Industry summaries of the settlement say the agreement must state the agent's compensation in a way that can be determined (a flat fee, percentage or hourly rate; summaries say an open-ended term like "whatever the seller offers" or a range does not qualify). The agent may not receive compensation from any source that exceeds the amount agreed in the buyer agreement. Offers of compensation can't be made on the MLS. These points are from trade-press summaries (RISMedia, Scotsman Guide) and not from the settlement text; **have your broker confirm**.
- **Negotiable:** Commissions are not set by law. A conspicuous statement that fees are negotiable is expected on agreements (confirm your state's required language).
- **Pushback:** The US Department of Justice filed a statement of interest in November 2024 raising antitrust concerns about the requirement of a written agreement before touring; the court approved the settlement anyway ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2024/12/02/court-report-nar-settlement-approved-but-doj-will-not-back-down/)). Rules continue to develop and some states have their own laws. Follow your broker's current policy.
- **Open houses:** Whether the agreement is needed for a visitor at a public open house is a question to ask your broker (unverified here). See [[Open House Playbook]].

**How to explain it** (example wording): "This agreement says what I do for you, how long it lasts, and how I'm paid. The fee is negotiable. If a seller pays some of it, it lowers what you pay. Let's walk through each part. You're welcome to have an attorney look at it."

Points to cover: term length, services, how and when you're paid, what happens if the seller offers less than agreed, termination, exclusivity, and what happens if they buy a property you showed them after the agreement ends. Use your broker's form.

## 3. Pre-approval
- Explain the difference: a *pre-qualification* is a quick estimate; a *pre-approval* usually involves a credit check and document review. Sellers usually take offers with a pre-approval more seriously. (General industry usage; terminology varies by lender.)
- Suggest the buyer talk to at least two lenders. You may share names of lenders but follow RESPA rules: do not accept referral fees for lender referrals. Ask your broker.
- Remind them: do not change jobs, open new credit or make large unexplained deposits before closing without asking the lender.
- Ask what monthly payment feels comfortable, not just what the lender approves.

## 4. Needs, wants and timeline
- **Needs vs. wants:** "If you could only have three things, what are they?"
- **Location:** commute, family, schools are preferences the *buyer* names. Do not steer or characterize neighborhoods by who lives there. See [[Fair Housing Advertising]].
- **Budget:** price, down payment, closing costs, monthly cost (taxes, insurance, HOA).
- **Timeline:** lease end, job move, school year, flexibility.
- **Dealbreakers:** condition, lot, parking, accessibility needs.
- **Decision makers:** who else needs to see and approve?

## 5. Explain the process
Walk through [[Transaction Timeline]]: search, offer, option or inspection period, appraisal, loan approval, Closing Disclosure (3 business days before closing), final walkthrough, closing.

Be honest about: competition in their price range, inspection findings, appraisal gaps, and that you can't predict the market.

## 6. Close the meeting
- Summarize what you heard; confirm next steps and a date for first tours.
- Send a follow-up email with the signed agreement copy, search link and checklist.
- Add them to your CRM with tasks. See [[Follow-Up Cadence]].

## Accessibility and language
Offer interpreters or translated materials when needed; see [[Serving Multilingual Clients]].
