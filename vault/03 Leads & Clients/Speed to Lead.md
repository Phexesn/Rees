# Speed to Lead

**In short:** Reaching a new online lead within minutes is associated with much better odds of making contact than waiting. The strongest-known studies are not real estate studies, and they are often misquoted. Treat "respond in 5 minutes" as a good habit backed by directional evidence, not a guarantee of results.

## What the research actually says

### 1. The 2007 "Lead Response Management" study (MIT / InsideSales.com)
- Presented in 2007 at a MarketingSherpa summit by James Oldroyd (then at MIT Sloan) and Dave Elkington (InsideSales.com). A copy of the slides is hosted at [mortech.com](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf).
- Widely repeated finding: the odds of **contacting** a lead were about **100x** higher if the first call attempt came within 5 minutes versus 30 minutes, and the odds of **qualifying** it were about **21x** higher. (Figures as summarized in secondary sources found by search; the original slides were not opened in this session.)
- Reported sample size varies by source (one says six companies, 15,000+ leads, 100,000+ call attempts; another says 100+ companies). Treat the sample as unclear.
- **Common mistakes when quoting it:**
  - It compares 5 minutes vs. **30** minutes, not 5 vs. 10.
  - It measured *contact and qualification*, not closed sales.
  - It is often credited to Harvard. The Harvard Business Review article is a different, later study.
  - It covered companies in general, not real estate agents specifically.

### 2. Harvard Business Review, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (2011)
- Authors: James B. Oldroyd, Kristina McElheran, David Elkington. Harvard Business Review, vol. 89 (March 2011), p. 28. Bibliographic record: [BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711).
- Secondary summaries report that the team sent test web leads to a large sample of US companies and measured response times; roughly a quarter took more than 24 hours and many never responded (one summary says 23%). One summary says leads were most likely to be qualified when contacted within 5 minutes. See, for example, [CBS News coverage](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/online-sales-leads-how-long-are-they-hot/) and [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/20110704-are-you-letting-online-leads-go-cold/). The HBR article is paywalled and could not be opened, so exact numbers are **unverified** here; check hbr.org before quoting a figure to clients or in marketing.

### 3. Real estate-specific consumer data
- NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers reports that 88% of buyers used an agent ([NAR summary](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/nar-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-sellers-reveals-market-extremes), from search summary).
- Secondary coverage of NAR data says most buyers speak to few agents: one article puts the share of buyers who hired the first agent they talked to at about 74%, while another reports 67% of first-time buyers and 76% of repeat buyers ([NowBAM, 74%](https://nowbam.com/74-of-buyers-hire-the-first-agent-they-talk-to-heres-how-to-be-that-agent/); [NowBAM, referral data](https://nowbam.com/how-home-buyers-and-sellers-find-their-agents-in-2025/)). These sources disagree (roughly 67-76%) and are trade-press summaries, so **verify against the NAR report** before citing. The takeaway holds either way: being the first helpful, professional person a buyer talks to matters.
- Some sources claim numbers like "over 40% of consumers want a response within hours." That comes from a vendor and is **unverified**.

## Practical rules for a newer agent
1. **Set alerts that you will actually see.** Push notifications from your CRM or portal app, on your phone, not just email.
2. **Aim to make first contact within minutes during working hours.** If you cannot, send a short text or email acknowledgment immediately (only where consent rules allow, see [[Texting & Calling Leads (TCPA)]]), then call as soon as you can.
3. **Call first, then text, then email.** A voice call is the fastest way to start a real conversation. Do not rely only on an auto-responder, which is not the same as a human reply.
4. **Be useful, not just fast.** A 2-minute call that answers the lead's question beats a 1-minute call that pitches.
5. **Log every attempt.** Note the time the lead came in and the time you responded so you can see your own average. Use [[Template - Weekly Business Review]].
6. **Evenings and weekends:** decide your own policy, and tell leads when to expect to hear from you. Set honest expectations ("I'll call you at 8 a.m. tomorrow").
7. **Know the rules before you call or text.** Prior express consent, Do Not Call lists and opt-outs apply. See [[Texting & Calling Leads (TCPA)]] and [[Email Marketing Rules (CAN-SPAM)]].
8. **Portal leads may have terms.** Portals often set expectations for response time in their agent agreements. Read yours.
