# Listing Appointment Guide

**In short:** A listing appointment is a job interview. The seller wants to know: what is my home likely to sell for, what will you do to sell it, what will I net, and can I trust you. Prepare a CMA, a marketing plan and a net sheet, and be honest about uncertainty.

## Before the appointment
- **Research the property:** public records, tax data, prior listings, permits (ask your broker which tools you can use).
- **Prepare a CMA** with sold, active/pending and expired comps. See [[CMA Basics]]. Present a *range*, not a promised price.
- **Check your broker's listing paperwork** and any state-required disclosures. Use [[Template - Seller Listing Checklist]].
- **Draft a net sheet** at two or three prices. See [[Commission & Net Sheet Basics]].
- **Prepare your marketing plan** (photos, listing description, signage, online syndication, open houses, social media). See [[Listing Description Guide]], [[Open House Playbook]] and [[Social Media Playbook]].
- **Confirm who attends** (all owners on title must usually sign).
- Ask in advance for the mortgage payoff estimate, repairs list and desired timing.

## Agenda (45-90 minutes)
1. **Get to know them:** why they are moving, timeline, what matters most.
2. **Walk the home:** note upgrades, condition and issues. Take notes honestly.
3. **Present you and your plan:** how you communicate, your process, your team.
4. **Market review:** inventory, days on market, recent sales (see [[CMA Basics]]).
5. **Pricing conversation:** below.
6. **Net proceeds:** go through the net sheet line by line.
7. **Compensation and listing agreement:** explain fees, term, cancellation, what is included.
8. **Next steps:** prep, photos date, paperwork.

## The pricing conversation
- Show comps and explain adjustments. Let the seller see the data.
- Explain the range: "Based on these sales, a likely range is $X to $Y. At the lower end, you'd expect a faster sale; at the higher end, you may wait longer or reduce later."
- A CMA is **not an appraisal**; it is an opinion based on comparable listings, and the final decision on list price is the seller's. Do not call it a "valuation" or "appraised value."
- Avoid "buying the listing" (overpromising to win it). Overpricing can result in more time on market. Avoid saying so as a guarantee; explain tradeoffs.
- Ask: "What price would make you happy? What would you net?" If their number is above the data, show the data kindly and propose a review date.

## Marketing plan: what to explain
- Pre-listing prep (decluttering, repairs, staging, cleaning, pre-inspection if relevant).
- Professional photos and video; a thorough, **accurate** description. Avoid discriminatory language ([[Fair Housing Advertising]]).
- MLS entry and syndication to portals; timing of go-live.
- Showings process and feedback.
- Offer review process and how you will negotiate.
- Compensation to buyer-side agents: since the settlement, offers of compensation can't be posted on the MLS; sellers may still negotiate offering concessions or compensation to buyer's agents outside the MLS. Explain the seller's options honestly. See [[NAR Settlement Changes]] and [[Commission & Net Sheet Basics]].

## Compensation conversation
- Commissions are negotiable. Be clear about what you charge and what that includes.
- Explain the listing agreement term, cancellation rights and any protection period clauses.
- If asked about a competitor's lower fee, see [[Objection Handling]].

## After the appointment
- Send a thank-you within 24 hours with the CMA, net sheet and next steps.
- Follow up on your cadence. See [[Follow-Up Cadence]].
- If you don't win it, ask what you could have done better. Stay in touch.
