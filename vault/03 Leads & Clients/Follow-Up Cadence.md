# Follow-Up Cadence

**In short:** Most leads are not ready on day one. A simple schedule of calls, texts and emails over 30 days, followed by light monthly or quarterly contact, keeps you in front of them without being annoying. The exact number of touches is a judgment call: published numbers come mainly from software vendors and coaches and are **not rigorous research**.

## What the data does and does not say
- Several vendor and coaching articles suggest 6-8 attempts over several weeks. One breakdown claims contact rates rise from 48% (1-3 attempts) to 93% (10+), but it names no study, so treat it as **unverified** ([Ylopo article](https://www.ylopo.com/ask-ylopo/how-often-contact-real-estate-leads), [Wise Agent](https://wiseagent.com/blog/4-real-estate-coaches-share-how-to-convert-an-internet-lead-via-phone); from search summary).
- The claim "average lead gets 1.8 attempts" is from one source and is **unverified**.
- The research that does exist is about *speed*; see [[Speed to Lead]].
- Do not use "80% of sales happen after the fifth contact" style statistics unless you can name the study. Many circulate without a source.

## Suggested 30-day plan (example)
Adjust to the lead's behavior. Stop when they ask you to, become a client, or choose another agent.

| Day | Channel | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (within minutes) | Call, then text, then email | Introduce yourself, answer their question, offer a next step. See [[Lead Follow-Up Scripts]] |
| 0 (evening) | Text or email | If no answer, short note with one helpful link |
| 1 | Call | Second attempt at a different time of day; leave a voicemail once |
| 3 | Email | Send a few matching listings or a local market snippet |
| 3 | Text | One-line check-in (only with consent) |
| 7 | Call | Ask if their timeline or plans have changed |
| 7 | Email | A guide: "How buying works here" or "Pricing your home" |
| 14 | Text and email | Offer a no-obligation chat; ask what would be helpful |
| 21 | Email | New listings or sold results near their search |
| 30 | Call and email | "Is it better to check in later?" Ask permission for monthly updates |

Not every lead needs every touch. If they reply, switch to their preferred channel and pace.

## After day 30: long-term nurture
- **Monthly:** a short email with market news, new listings that fit them, or a local event.
- **Quarterly:** a personal call or text. For past clients: home value update, anniversary of closing, a handwritten note.
- **Annual:** a "home-iversary" note. Past clients are your referral source: secondary coverage of NAR data says 43% of buyers used an agent found via referral and 18% used one they had worked with before ([NowBAM](https://nowbam.com/how-home-buyers-and-sellers-find-their-agents-in-2025/); verify in NAR's report).
- **Sort leads:** hot (ready within 3 months), warm (3-12 months), long-term (over 12 months). Hot ones get calls; others get content.

## Rules that limit your cadence
- **Consent and opt-outs.** Calls and texts need proper consent; honor "stop" immediately. See [[Texting & Calling Leads (TCPA)]].
- **Email:** include an unsubscribe method and your physical address; honor opt-outs. See [[Email Marketing Rules (CAN-SPAM)]].
- **Do Not Call:** do not call numbers on the Registry without an exemption or permission (see [[Lead Follow-Up Scripts]]).
- **Quiet hours:** do not call at odd hours. Federal telemarketing rules generally prohibit telemarketing calls before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the called party's time zone (general knowledge; confirm with your broker).
- **Fair treatment:** use the same cadence and content standards for all leads, regardless of protected class.

## Making it stick
- Set tasks in your CRM; do not rely on memory.
- Log each touch and the outcome.
- Review weekly with [[Template - Weekly Business Review]].
- Vary the content. Offer something useful each time (a listing, a rate update, a checklist).
