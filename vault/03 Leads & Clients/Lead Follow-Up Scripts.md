# Lead Follow-Up Scripts

**In short:** Original, plain-language examples for six common lead types. Each has an opening, discovery questions, an appointment ask, a voicemail, a text and an email. Adapt to your own voice. These are **examples written for this playbook**, not legal advice. Have your broker review anything you put in writing or send in bulk.

## Before you use any script
- **Calling and texting leads is regulated.** Check consent, Do Not Call status and opt-out handling first: [[Texting & Calling Leads (TCPA)]]. Emails need to follow [[Email Marketing Rules (CAN-SPAM)]].
- **Identify yourself and your brokerage** in the first sentence. Many states require your brokerage name in advertising and some communications; ask your broker.
- **Treat everyone the same.** Ask about needs and budget, not about family status, religion, national origin or similar. See [[Fair Housing Advertising]].
- **Speed matters.** See [[Speed to Lead]].
- Replace [brackets] with your details. Keep texts short.

## 1. Portal inquiry (Zillow, Realtor.com, etc.)
The lead clicked "Is this home still available?" or "Contact agent" on one specific listing.

**Opening (call):**
"Hi [Name], this is [You] with [Brokerage]. You asked about [address] a few minutes ago, so I wanted to get you an answer quickly. Is now a good moment for two minutes?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "What caught your eye about that home?"
2. "Are you hoping to see it in person, or still comparing options online?"
3. "Where else are you looking, and what would make you cross a place off?"
4. "What timeline are you working with for a move?"
5. "Have you talked with a lender yet, or is that still ahead?" (Say why you ask: "so I can tell you which homes make sense to spend time on.")

**Ask for the appointment:** "I can check the showing availability for [address]. Would [day] at [time] or [day] at [time] work to see it? If it's not right, I can line up two or three similar ones the same day."
*Note:* Many areas now expect a written buyer agreement before touring. Mention it early and calmly: "Before we tour, I'll walk you through a short agreement that explains how I work and how I'm paid. It's all negotiable and I'll explain every line." See [[Buyer Consultation Guide]].

**Voicemail (under 25 seconds):** "Hi [Name], it's [You] with [Brokerage], calling about your question on [address]. I have a quick answer and a couple of similar homes. Call or text me back at [number]. Again, [You], [number]."

**Follow-up text:** "Hi [Name], it's [You] at [Brokerage]. I tried you about [address]. Happy to answer questions or set up a showing. When's a good time to chat? Reply STOP to opt out." (Opt-out language: confirm with your broker.)

**Follow-up email:**
Subject: About [address]
"Hi [Name], thanks for asking about [address]. Here is what I know so far: [two facts, e.g., status, an upcoming open house]. If you'd like to see it, I can arrange a time this week. A quick call would help me point you to the right homes. What time suits you? [Signature with license number, brokerage]"

## 2. Open-house sign-in
(Full set of post-event messages is in [[Open House Follow-Up]]; see also [[Open House Playbook]].)

**Opening (call within 24 hours):** "Hi [Name], this is [You] from [Brokerage]. We met at the open house at [address] on [day]. I wanted to say thanks for stopping by. Do you have a minute?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "What did you like most about the home? What was a dealbreaker, if anything?"
2. "Are you working with an agent already?" (If yes, respect it and wish them well. Do not try to persuade them to break an agreement.)
3. "What's your rough timeline?"
4. "What areas are you comparing?"
5. "What would you want to see next?"

**Ask for the appointment:** "If you'd like, I can send a short list of homes that fit what you described, and we could tour two or three on [day]."

**Voicemail:** "Hi [Name], [You] from [Brokerage]. Thanks for visiting [address] on [day]. If you have questions about the home or the area, I'm at [number]."

**Text (only if the sign-in sheet gave texting consent):** "Hi [Name], it's [You] from the [address] open house. Thanks for coming by. Any questions I can answer? Reply STOP to opt out."

**Email:** Subject: Thanks for visiting [address]. Body: thank them, add 2-3 facts about the home or neighborhood, offer to send similar listings, include how to unsubscribe.

## 3. Sign call (someone calls the number on your yard sign)

**Opening:** "[Brokerage], this is [You]. Thanks for calling about [address]."

**Discovery questions:**
1. "Have you seen the home, or are you calling from the sign?"
2. "What would you like to know first?"
3. "Are you looking to buy, or are you curious about the neighborhood in general?"
4. "Is there a time frame for your move?"
5. "May I get your name and the best number in case we are cut off?"

**Ask for the appointment:** "I can show it [day/time] or send you the details now. Which is better?"

**Voicemail (if they hang up and you call back):** "Hi, this is [You] with [Brokerage]. You called about the [address] sign. I'm sorry I missed you. Call me at [number] and I'll get you the details."

**Text:** "Hi, it's [You] at [Brokerage] returning your call about [address]. Want details or a showing? Reply STOP to opt out."

**Email:** Short listing summary with a link, your contact details, and an offer to show.

## 4. Website or landing-page form
The form should state clearly how you will contact them, and what you will send (for example, "I agree to be contacted by call, text and email"). Ask your broker to approve the exact wording and consider [[Texting & Calling Leads (TCPA)]].

**Opening:** "Hi [Name], it's [You] with [Brokerage]. You filled out a form on my site about [topic: home search / home value]. I'm calling to see how I can help. Is now OK?"

**Discovery questions (buyer):**
1. "What area and price range are you thinking about?"
2. "What's most important in a home?" (Needs first, nice-to-haves second.)
3. "When would you like to be in a new place?"
4. "Is there anything about the process that feels confusing?"
5. "Have you spoken with a lender?"

**Discovery questions (seller / home-value form):**
1. "What's prompting you to think about selling?"
2. "When would you like to be moved?"
3. "What have you done to the home that you are proud of?"
4. "What would you need to net to make a move work?"
5. "Would you like me to walk through it and prepare a pricing range?" (A CMA is not an appraisal; see [[CMA Basics]].)

**Ask for the appointment:** "The easiest next step is a 20-minute call or visit. Would [day] or [day] be better?"

**Voicemail:** "Hi [Name], [You] from [Brokerage]. Thanks for your request on my website. I'd like to help. Reach me at [number]."

**Text:** "Hi [Name], it's [You] from [Brokerage] following up on your request. What's a good time for a quick call? Reply STOP to opt out."

**Email:** Subject: Your [home search / home value] request. Restate what they asked, give one useful item (a market note, a neighborhood link), offer two times to talk, include unsubscribe link and physical address (CAN-SPAM).

## 5. Past-client check-in
Past clients and your sphere are the most receptive audience, and referrals are important in this business. Secondary coverage of the NAR 2025 Profile says that 43% of buyers found their agent through a referral and 18% used an agent they had worked with before ([NowBAM summary](https://nowbam.com/how-home-buyers-and-sellers-find-their-agents-in-2025/); verify in the NAR report).

**Opening:** "Hi [Name], it's [You]. I was thinking about you and your home on [street]. It's been about [time] since closing. How's the house treating you?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "Anything come up with the home I can help find a vendor for?"
2. "How is the neighborhood treating you?"
3. "Have you been curious about your home's value? I'm happy to send a quick update."
4. "Is anyone you know thinking about buying or selling?"
5. "What would make my follow-ups more useful to you?"

**Ask:** "If you ever want to know what your home would sell for today, I can put together a short report. No pressure."

**Voicemail:** "Hi [Name], [You] here. Just checking in, no need to call back unless you want to. If you need anything, I'm at [number]."

**Text:** "Hi [Name], it's [You]. Happy [anniversary of your closing / season]! Let me know if you need anything for the house."

**Email:** Short personal note, a one-paragraph local market update, and a line like: "If anyone you know is thinking of a move, I'd be glad to help."

## 6. Expired listing / For Sale By Owner (FSBO)
**Read this first:** Cold-calling these owners can violate the National Do Not Call Registry rules. Trade-association and legal summaries say the federal rules generally bar calls to numbers on the Registry unless you have an existing business relationship or the person's written permission; the FCC declined to create an exemption for expired listings, and an agent representing a buyer may call a FSBO only about a potential sale to that buyer, not to solicit a listing ([Florida Realtors](https://floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/library/do-not-call); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/real-estate/1494276/can-realtors-call-fsbo-sellers-on-the-do-not-call-list); [EPGD Law](https://www.epgdlaw.com/can-realtors-call-fsbo-sellers-on-the-do-not-call-list/)). Some sources claim the original listing agent has an established relationship for 18 months after expiration; that applies to that brokerage's relationship, not to other agents. Rules also vary by state. **Ask your broker; scrub numbers against the Registry and your internal do-not-call list; never use these scripts on a number you are not allowed to call.** See [[Texting & Calling Leads (TCPA)]].

Lower-risk options: a handwritten note, a mailed letter, or a door visit where permitted (check local solicitation rules and "no soliciting" signs).

**Opening (if permitted):** "Hi [Name], this is [You] with [Brokerage]. I saw your home at [address] came off the market. I'm not calling to criticize, just to see if you still plan to move. Is this a bad time?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "What happened while it was listed, in your view?"
2. "Do you still want to move, or have plans changed?"
3. "What feedback did you get from showings?"
4. "What would you want a new plan to include?"
5. "Are you already working with another agent?" (If yes, thank them and end the call.)

**Ask for the appointment:** "I'd like to look at the home and review the market data with you. It takes 30 minutes and there is no obligation. Would [day] work?"

**Voicemail:** "Hi, [You] from [Brokerage]. I'm reaching out about [address]. If you'd like a second opinion on selling, call me at [number]. If you'd rather not hear from me, tell me and I'll take you off my list."

**Follow-up text:** Skip unless you have consent.

**Follow-up email/letter:** Short, non-critical, no promises about price or time on market. Do not criticize the previous agent. Do not interfere with an existing listing agreement.

## Tone rules that apply to every script
- Never promise a price, a speed of sale or a mortgage outcome.
- Don't pressure. A "no" is a valid answer; log it and respect it.
- If someone says "stop," record it right away and stop contacting them that way.
- Keep notes of what they said, so the next call sounds like a continuation.
