# Transaction Timeline

**In short:** From accepted offer to closing, a financed purchase often takes roughly 30 to 60 days, with big differences by state, contract, loan type and market. The timeline below is a general guide. **Your state's contract and dates control.** Always use the dates in the signed contract.

## Typical timing evidence
- A GoBankingRates article reports ICE Mortgage Technology data putting the average time to close at about 43 days as of February 2025, and typical closings at 30 to 60 days; it also gives underwriting 5-20 days and says appraisals and title searches can each take up to about two weeks ([GoBankingRates](https://www.gobankingrates.com/home-loans/home-loans-advice/how-long-does-it-take-to-close-on-a-house/); from search summary; I could not find ICE's own release). Older ICE reports (2021) showed about 51 days.
- **Closing Disclosure:** For most mortgage loans, the lender must ensure the borrower receives the Closing Disclosure at least **three business days before closing** (consummation). For this rule, business days generally include Saturdays but not Sundays or federal holidays (per third-party guides). Some changes (certain APR increases beyond tolerance, change in loan product, adding a prepayment penalty) require a corrected disclosure and a new three-business-day wait. CFPB says that if you don't get one, ask the lender and don't close until you've received and reviewed it. Sources: [CFPB Ask](https://consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-i-do-not-get-a-closing-disclosure-three-days-before-my-mortgage-closing-en-1911) and [ALTA](https://www.alta.org/blog/post/how-to-comply-with-the-closing-disclosures-three-day-rule) (search summaries). The rule is in Regulation Z (TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure). Regulations can change; check the CFPB for the current text.

## Buyer-side timeline (example, financed purchase)
Day numbers count from the **effective date** of the contract and are **examples**.

| Step | Typical timing (varies) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Offer accepted; contract signed | Day 0 | Confirm effective date and every deadline |
| Earnest money deposit | Often within 1-3 business days | Amount and deadline are in the contract |
| Option or inspection period | Commonly 5-15 days (state/contract dependent; e.g., a Texas-style option period is negotiated) | Order inspections right away; negotiate repairs or credits |
| Loan application / disclosures | Within 3 business days of application, lender gives the Loan Estimate | Lender sends disclosures |
| Appraisal ordered and completed | Often 1-3 weeks | Lender orders; low appraisal may mean renegotiation |
| Title search, survey, insurance | Parallel, up to a couple weeks | Title company or attorney (depends on state) |
| Financing contingency deadline | Often 3-5 weeks | Per contract |
| Underwriting / clear to close | Often 1-3 weeks | |
| Closing Disclosure received | At least 3 business days before closing | |
| Final walkthrough | Within 24-48 hours of closing (typical practice) | Verify condition and agreed repairs |
| Closing / funding / recording | Day ~30-60 | Sign; keys per contract and state law |

## Seller-side timeline
| Step | Notes |
|---|---|
| Accept offer | Review all terms, deadlines and contingencies |
| Earnest money confirmed | Confirm receipt |
| Inspection negotiations | Respond within contract deadlines |
| Appraisal | Be ready for access; plan for a low appraisal |
| Disclosures | Deliver required seller disclosures (state-specific) |
| Title and payoff | Request payoff statement; clear liens |
| Repairs | Complete agreed repairs with receipts |
| Final walkthrough access | Home vacant and clean per contract |
| Closing | Sign, receive proceeds per state method |

## Things that change dates
- State rules (attorney-closing states vs. escrow states, option period vs. attorney review).
- Loan type (FHA, VA, conventional, cash). Cash deals can close faster.
- Appraisal or repair delays, title issues, lender backlogs.
- Contract extensions must be in writing, signed by all parties.

## Your role
- Create a calendar of every deadline on day one and share it with the client. Use [[Template - Weekly Business Review]] for your pipeline.
- Explain each step in plain language ([[Buyer Consultation Guide]]).
- Communicate promptly with lender, title and the other agent.
- Get legal or broker advice before giving advice on contract interpretation.
