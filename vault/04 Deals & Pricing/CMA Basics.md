# CMA Basics

**In short:** A Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) is an agent's opinion of a likely price *range* for a home, based on similar properties that sold, are for sale, or failed to sell. It is **not an appraisal**. Appraisals are done by state-licensed or certified appraisers following USPAP. Say "suggested price range," not "value."

## CMA vs. appraisal vs. BPO
| | CMA | BPO (Broker Price Opinion) | Appraisal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Real estate licensee | Licensee (rules vary by state) | State-licensed or certified appraiser |
| Standard | Not required to follow USPAP | Varies by state; often required disclaimers | USPAP, published by the Appraisal Foundation |
| Purpose | Pricing a listing or offer | Often lender/asset-manager use | Lending, tax, legal and other defined purposes |
| Output | Price range and reasoning | Opinion of price | Opinion of value |

Supporting points (from search summaries):
- USPAP sets ethics and performance rules for appraisers and is published by the Appraisal Foundation. A licensee's CMA is not an appraisal and is not required to comply with USPAP.
- For federally related transactions, only a state-licensed or certified appraiser may appraise.
- Florida Realtors notes that a BPO or CMA by someone who is not a licensed/certified appraiser can't be called an "appraisal" ([Florida Realtors](https://floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/library/Appraisals)).
- New Jersey's banking regulator has published guidance that unlicensed BPO preparers may be engaged in unlicensed appraisal ([NJ DOBI bulletin](https://nj.gov/dobi/bulletins/blt13_05.pdf)); Idaho's guideline calls for a BPO disclaimer saying it is not intended to meet USPAP ([Idaho DOPL](https://dopl.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/REC-Guideline-20-rev-7.11.pdf)); Oregon's Real Estate Agency has a page on CMA and BPO requirements ([Oregon REA](https://www.oregon.gov/rea/newsroom/Pages/2026_OREN-J/CMA-and-BPO-Requirements.aspx)); Texas has a TREC rule on CMA/BPO disclaimers (cited as 535.17 in search snippets; wording not retrieved) ([Realty Texas](https://www.realtytexas.com/blog/10418/what-is-a-bpo-or-cma-under-texas-law.html)). **Your state's rule governs.** Check with your commission and broker.

## Step-by-step
1. **Define the subject:** address, beds/baths, size (gross living area), lot, age, condition, upgrades, HOA, view, garage.
2. **Pick comparable sales (comps).** Common criteria (rules of thumb, **not authoritative**; markets differ):
   - **Status:** use *sold* as primary evidence. Active and pending listings show competition; expired/withdrawn show what the market rejected.
   - **Distance:** same subdivision or neighborhood first; widen only if needed.
   - **Recency:** commonly within 3-6 months, but depends on market speed.
   - **Size:** similar living area (often within ~10-20%).
   - **Type and style:** same property type (single-family vs. condo), similar age and style.
   - **Condition, lot, bed/bath, garage, HOA.**
   - Number: commonly 3-6 sold comps plus a few active/pending for context (rule of thumb; [HouseCanary](https://www.housecanary.com/blog/how-to-do-a-cma), search result).
3. **Adjust for differences.** If a comp has a feature the subject lacks, adjust the comp's price down; if the subject has more, adjust up. Use dollar adjustments backed by paired sales or local data. Rule-of-thumb figures found on blogs (e.g., per-square-foot, bathroom, garage, pool values) conflict and **should not be used as defaults**; derive from your local market. Adjust for time (market change) as a separate line.
4. **Weigh comps:** closest, most recent, most similar count more.
5. **Check competition:** active/pending listings and expired listings (overpricing signal).
6. **Present a range:** low, target, high, with reasoning. Let the client make the final pricing decision.
7. **Document:** MLS numbers, dates, data sources, adjustments, the date you pulled the data.

## Cautions
- **Never invent or round data**; every figure should trace to an MLS or public record.
- Condition is subjective: document what you saw.
- Do not describe neighborhoods by who lives there; stick to property facts ([[Fair Housing Advertising]]).
- MLS data use rules limit how you can display or share data. Check your MLS rules.
- If software or AI produces a draft, review and approve every adjustment yourself.
- Don't use the CMA as a lender valuation or call it "market value" or "appraised value."

## Sample disclaimer (example only: **have your broker approve**; state-specific wording may be required)
> **Comparative Market Analysis. Not an Appraisal.** Prepared by [Agent name], licensed real estate [salesperson/broker] in [State], License No. [###], with [Brokerage], on [date]. Based on information from [MLS / public records] believed reliable but not independently verified. This is not an appraisal, was not prepared under the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP), and is not a valuation for lending, tax or any other purpose. It states a suggested price range based on comparable properties and the preparer's opinion. Pricing decisions are the owner's. Obtain a licensed or certified appraiser's opinion if needed.
