# Commission & Net Sheet Basics

**In short:** Real estate commissions are negotiable. Since the NAR settlement practice changes (effective August 17, 2024), offers of compensation can't be advertised on MLSs, and buyer-agent compensation is set in a written agreement with the buyer. A net sheet estimates what a seller keeps after costs. The numbers below are **fake examples**.

## How commissions work after the settlement
- **Negotiable, not set by law.** NAR's consumer materials say commissions are negotiable and not set by law ([NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-written-buyer-agreements); search summary).
- **Seller pays listing broker** per the listing agreement. The seller may also choose to offer compensation or concessions to a buyer's agent, but offers of compensation can't be displayed on the MLS; they happen off-MLS (website, direct communication, or concessions in the offer). See [[NAR Settlement Changes]].
- **Buyer pays buyer's agent per the buyer agreement.** Buyer-agent pay can come from fixed-fee amounts paid by the buyer, seller concessions, or a portion of the listing broker's compensation. A buyer-agent can't receive more than the amount agreed in the buyer agreement (per NAR/state association summaries; see [[Buyer Consultation Guide]]).
- **Brokerage splits:** Your pay comes from your brokerage. Your split with your broker is set by your independent contractor agreement (e.g., 70/30, 100% with a fee, caps). There is no national standard. Read yours and ask what fees apply (desk fee, transaction fee, E&O).
- **Data:** Redfin reported the average buyer's-agent commission was 2.42% in Q3 2025, up from 2.36% a year earlier (Q3 2024), with higher percentages for homes under $500,000 (2.52%) and lower for $1 million or more (2.22%). Redfin's figures exclude deals where its own agents represented the buyer ([Redfin May 2025](https://www.redfin.com/news/real-estate-commissions-may-2025/), [Redfin Q3 2025 PDF](https://www.redfin.com/news/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Press-Release_Q3-commissions.pdf), [MortgagePoint](https://themortgagepoint.com/2025/12/31/buyers-agent-commissions-inch-up-as-slower-market-shifts-leverage-to-buyers/); search summaries). I found no similar listing-side average and did not find Real Trends data.
- **Antitrust caution:** Never agree with other brokers on commission rates, and never say rates are "standard." Each is negotiated.

## What a net sheet shows
An estimate of the seller's proceeds: sale price minus mortgage payoff, commission(s), closing costs, taxes, repairs and credits. It is an **estimate**, not a promise. Title or escrow companies or attorneys can produce more accurate versions.

## Example (all numbers fictional)
Sale price: $400,000

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale price | $400,000 |
| Listing broker commission (example 2.5%) | - $10,000 |
| Buyer-agent compensation by seller (example 2.0%, if the seller agreed to offer it) | - $8,000 |
| Seller concession to buyer (closing costs, example) | - $3,000 |
| Title insurance / escrow / attorney fees (example) | - $2,500 |
| Transfer taxes and recording (example; varies widely by state) | - $1,500 |
| Prorated property tax owed (example) | - $1,200 |
| Repairs agreed after inspection (example) | - $1,800 |
| Mortgage payoff (example) | - $210,000 |
| **Estimated net proceeds** | **$162,000** |

Check: deductions total $28,000 in costs plus the $210,000 payoff = $238,000; $400,000 - $238,000 = $162,000.

Try a low-price version, and a high-price version, to show sensitivity.

## Tips
- Use percentages only as examples. Fill in your broker's real rates and local fee schedules.
- Include line items the seller might forget: HOA transfer fees, home warranty, utility proration, moving costs (optional).
- Update the net sheet when the offer comes in. Show the net for each offer, since terms (concessions, repairs, timing) change the number.
- State which items are estimates.
- If the seller will offer compensation to buyer's agents, explain that it is optional and negotiable and how it is handled in your market ([[Listing Appointment Guide]]).
- Taxes: don't give tax advice; refer to a CPA about capital gains.
