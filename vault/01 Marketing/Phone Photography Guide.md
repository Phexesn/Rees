# Phone Photography Guide

**In short:** A recent phone can take good listing photos if you control four things: where you stand (chest height, level, in a corner), the light (all lamps on, shoot when the room is bright but not blasted by direct sun), the clutter (remove it first), and the settings (ultra-wide used sparingly, grid on, exposure locked on the room, not the window). Editing is a light touch afterwards ([[Phone Photo Editing (Free Apps)]]). Hire a pro for luxury homes, twilight, or when your MLS or seller expects it.

## 1. Settings that matter

| Setting | What to do | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Lens | Use the **0.5x ultra-wide** for small rooms; switch to **1x** when edges look stretched or for detail shots. Ultra-wide bends straight lines near the frame edges, so keep the room's key furniture away from the corners. | Guides recommend 0.5x as the first choice for interiors and warn about distortion (photoworkout.com, boxbrownie.com, see Sources). Advice to prefer 1x on tight rooms: common practice |
| Grid | **iPhone:** Settings > Camera > turn on Grid, and turn on **Level** under Composition. **Pixel:** Camera > Settings > More settings > Composition > Grid type (3x3, 4x4, golden ratio) and Framing hints (level line). **Samsung:** Camera > Settings > Grid lines on older One UI; on One UI 7 and later the option is called **Composition guide**. | iPhone: secondary sources (slashgear, ioshacker). Pixel: Android Authority. Samsung: Samsung support pages. Names move between versions |
| Level | The iPhone Level shows when the phone is flat or aimed at a level target. Pixel shows a level line when Framing hints is on. | Secondary sources |
| Exposure lock | **iPhone:** tap and hold on the room (not the window) until "AE/AF Lock" appears, then drag the sun icon to brighten or darken. **Pixel:** long-press the viewfinder to lock focus and exposure; adjust with the slider. **Samsung:** press and hold the preview; some older manuals say AE/AF lock is limited to Pro mode. | iPhone: iPhoneLife and other tutorials (Apple's own page I found covers the Camera Control button only). Pixel: summary of help-site articles. Samsung: older manual, check your model |
| HDR | Leave automatic HDR on. If windows look glowing or halos appear, reduce the edit strength (see editing note) or re-shoot with a darker exposure. Apple's HDR settings guide says HDR is not user-adjustable on iPhone 13 and later; older models had a Smart HDR toggle and "Keep Normal Photo". | Apple guide (via search summary); halo remedy is common practice |
| Flash | Off. | common practice |
| Live Photo / Portrait / Cinematic / Night mode | Off for listing stills. Night mode can soften detail and shift colors. | common practice |
| View Outside the Frame (iPhone) | Optional. It records extra area for later cropping; sources disagree on whether it is on by default. | search summary, conflicting |
| Resolution / format | Standard JPEG/HEIC is fine. RAW only if you will edit in a RAW app. | common practice |
| Timer | Use a 2-second timer or remote when on a tripod. | common practice |

**Pro modes:** Samsung "Pro mode" and Expert RAW, and Pixel's Pro controls, give manual control. iOS's built-in Camera app has no manual ISO or shutter control (third-party apps do). Details by model were not verified here (unverified).

## 2. Camera height, framing, shooting order

- **Height:** about chest or waist height, roughly 4 feet is the figure several guides give. Lower makes ceilings feel taller; much higher shows too much floor. (common practice; photoworkout.com and others suggest about waist height)
- **Level the phone** so vertical lines (door frames, corners) stay vertical. Do not tilt up to "get the ceiling".
- **Shoot from corners or doorways** so you show two walls, depth, and flow.
- **Horizontal frames** for MLS/portals unless your MLS says otherwise.
- **Take two or three frames per angle** and pick the best later.

**Shooting order (works for any size):**
1. Front exterior (street view, straight on).
2. Entry.
3. Living room, then kitchen, then dining.
4. Primary bedroom and bath.
5. Other bedrooms and baths.
6. Yard, patio, pool, garage.
7. One or two detail shots (fireplace, view, new feature).
8. Pick your strongest image as the lead photo.

MLS photo counts and minimums differ by system. Examples from MLS help pages, via search snippets in earlier research: Doorify MLS requires at least 3 photos (maximum 100), Unlock MLS allows at most 40, MLS Now at most 50, GBRAR at least 5 for non-new-construction residential. Check your own MLS (search-snippet level).

## 3. Light: time of day and room direction

All times are rough starting points; check sun direction with a free sun-path app on the day. (common practice)

| Room or wall faces | Brightest, most useful light | Note |
|---|---|---|
| East | Morning | Low warm sun can blast windows; lower exposure a little |
| South | Midday to early afternoon | Strongest light; control glare with sheer curtains or lowered blinds |
| West | Afternoon | Can be harsh and warm in summer |
| North | All day, soft | Even but dim; turn on every light |

- **Front exterior:** put the sun behind you and on the facade. For a north-facing front, try an overcast day for soft light.
- **Twilight:** 15 to 30 minutes after sunset, all interior and exterior lights on, phone on a tripod. Hire a pro if you are unsure.
- **Windows:** shoot rooms with the least direct sun on the glass. Overcast days are forgiving.
- **Light bulbs:** replace burned-out ones and match color (all warm or all neutral).
- **Blinds and curtains:** open fully unless the view is poor; tilt blinds to soften light.

## 4. Staging checklist (use before every shoot)

- [ ] Every light and lamp on; blinds open; ceiling fans off.
- [ ] Remove personal photos, mail, magnets and notes on the fridge.
- [ ] Clear counters except one or two items.
- [ ] Toilet lids down; remove toiletries from tub and counter; fresh towels.
- [ ] Beds made; laundry gone; closet doors closed.
- [ ] Hide cords, pet bowls, toys, trash cans, shoes at the door.
- [ ] Cars off the driveway and street in front of the house.
- [ ] Lawn mowed, hoses coiled, bins out of sight, doormat straight.
- [ ] Pool skimmed and equipment tucked away.
- [ ] Walk the shot list once before shooting to spot what the camera will see.

Staging should show the real room. Do not move things that hide defects, and do not claim staged furniture comes with the home. See [[Listing Description Guide]] for disclosure language.

## 5. Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leaning vertical lines | Hold the phone level; use grid and level; fix in an editor |
| Blown-out windows | Lock exposure on the room and lower brightness slightly; shoot at softer light; do not chase a perfect window view on the phone |
| Dark, yellow rooms | Turn on all lights; match bulbs; add a small LED panel ([[Gear Under $100]]) |
| Mixed color casts (window blue plus lamp orange) | Turn lamps off or all on; fix white balance in editing |
| Fisheye edges | Step back, switch to 1x, or keep objects away from corners |
| Photographer visible in mirrors | Shoot at an angle; stand to the side; take a clean frame and verify at full size |
| Blurry low-light shots | Tripod plus timer; more light; do not use Night mode with moving subjects |
| Tilted horizon outdoors | Use grid; straighten in editing |
| Messy yard or cars | Fix in real life; do not remove with editing (see editing note for what is allowed) |
| Lens smudges | Wipe the lens before every room |

## 6. DIY vs. hiring a pro

- Many vendor guides quote roughly $150 to $300 for a standard residential shoot, with a wider $100 to $500 range depending on size and location. Those are vendor numbers; get local quotes. (homejab.com, thumbtack.com, see Sources.)
- A 2013 Redfin study found professional DSLR photos were associated with faster sales and higher sale-to-list ratios. It is old and correlational; the details come from a search summary of the Redfin and PR Newswire releases and I did not open Redfin's page. Do not promise sellers a specific outcome.
- Consider a pro for luxury listings, twilight, drone shots, unusual lighting, or when the seller wants it. Commercial drone work in the US generally requires an FAA Part 107 certificate (vendor sources; confirm on faa.gov, which I could not open).

## 7. Quick plan: 20-minute shoot

1. 0-5 min: lights on, blinds open, quick declutter, cars moved.
2. 5-8 min: exterior (3-5 frames).
3. 8-17 min: interiors, 2-3 frames per room, corners and doorways.
4. 17-20 min: backyard and a couple of details, review at full size on the phone.
5. Edit at home with [[Phone Photo Editing (Free Apps)]].
