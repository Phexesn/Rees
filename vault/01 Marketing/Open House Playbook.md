# Open House Playbook

**In short:** A good open house is prepared in the week before, run safely and with visible sign-in on the day, and followed up the same day. Bring a second person if you can, know your exits, use a sign-in with a plain consent line for follow-up, check every room before you lock up, and start follow-up within hours. Open house details belong in your MLS's open-house fields and your social posts, not in the public remarks.

> Verification status: NAR's safety pages could not be opened directly (host not reachable); the safety tips below come from search summaries of NAR's "10 tips for holding a safe open house" and other safety pages. Follow-up timing and sign-in language come from vendor and association blogs. Texting consent is a legal area: confirm wording with your broker or an attorney.

## 1. Before the open house (checklist)

- [ ] Get the seller's written OK on date, time, and rules (valuables put away, pets handled, security cameras disclosed).
- [ ] Enter the open house in the MLS open-house fields; keep time details out of public remarks.
- [ ] Check your broker's and state's rules for open house signage and advertising.
- [ ] Promote it: Facebook event, Instagram story and Reel, directional signs, a neighbor flyer drop. Plan in [[Social Media Playbook]].
- [ ] Clean and stage: lights on, blinds open, clutter gone, fresh smell (no heavy fragrance), trash out. Use the checklist in [[Phone Photography Guide]].
- [ ] Prepare a feature sheet (price, key facts, nearby info) with no unverified claims; include brokerage name.
- [ ] Prepare sign-in sheet or tablet app; print extra copies.
- [ ] Safety walk-through: locate exits, check that deadbolts and back doors open easily, make sure the phone has a signal in the house, note where you can see the front door from.
- [ ] Tell a colleague or friend where you are and when you expect to finish; share your location.
- [ ] Tell a neighbor if appropriate (NAR's list suggests this).
- [ ] Plan for a second person; NAR's top tip is to have at least one other person with you if possible.
- [ ] Pack: charger, water, pens, feature sheets, business cards, name tag, shoe covers or a mat.

## 2. During the open house

- Greet at the door; ask everyone to sign in. A sign-in is also a safety record.
- Keep a light, short conversation; ask open questions ("What brings you to this neighborhood?").
- Do not hover. Offer to answer questions, then give space.
- Keep valuables, prescription drugs and small items put away; watch the bedrooms and bathrooms. Anecdotes in agent videos describe visitors slipping into rooms while the host is distracted.
- Park where you will not be blocked in; keep keys and your phone on you, not in a bag across the house.
- Note buyers' needs (timeline, price range, pre-approval, what they need to see) on the sign-in or in your phone straight after the visit.
- Remember Fair Housing: do not steer or comment on neighborhood demographics, schools, or "who lives here" ([[Fair Housing Advertising]]).

### Sign-in best practices

1. Ask for name, phone, email and whether they are working with an agent. Asking for a driver's licence or address is optional; if you ask, say why and keep the data secure.
2. **Consent line:** put a plain sentence on the sheet saying the person agrees to be contacted by you (name and brokerage) about this property and similar homes by phone, text and email, and that message rates may apply. Use a separate, unchecked-by-default box for text messages. NAR has written that this consent can be built into methods such as open house sign-in sheets, and vendor guidance recommends written, timestamped consent kept on file. Because wording matters, have your broker approve it. (See [[Lead Follow-Up Scripts]].)
3. Never put visitors on a mailing list for unrelated products without telling them.
4. Keep the sheet private: use a clipboard with a cover, a tablet, or an app so earlier visitors' details are not visible to later ones.
5. Store the data safely and delete or stop contacting anyone who asks.
6. Do not share the list with others (lenders, vendors) unless your sign-in said you would and your broker agrees.

Reported penalty figures for unconsented texts are commonly cited as $500-$1,500 per violation in vendor blogs; I could not verify this from an official source. Treat it as a reason to be careful, not as a precise number.

## 3. Safety (NAR-based)

From NAR's "10 tips for holding a safe open house" (via search summary) and related NAR safety pages:

- Try always to have at least one other person with you.
- Have visitors sign in.
- Tell a neighbor you will be showing the home and ask them to watch for anything unusual.
- Know the layout and at least two exits; make sure deadbolts can be opened quickly.
- At the end, do not assume everyone has left; check all rooms and the backyard before locking up.
- Keep your phone charged and with you; share your location with someone.
- Trust your instincts: if someone makes you uneasy, leave and call for help.

NAR also runs a Safety site (webinars, a Safety Resource Kit, and an incident-reporting form for members) and designates September as REALTOR(R) Safety Month (search summary). Start at https://www.nar.realtor/safety and check the dates yourself. Several NAR pages carried a "may not be current" notice in search results.

Your brokerage may have its own policy (for example, two-person open houses or check-in calls). Follow it.

## 4. After the open house

**Same day**
- [ ] Sweep the property; lock up; verify nothing is missing.
- [ ] Send the seller a short report: count, comments, price feedback.
- [ ] Send a thank-you message to visitors who gave consent. Vendor guides recommend starting within hours (Serif suggests 4-6 hours; another vendor suggests within 2 hours). These are vendor suggestions, not studies.
- [ ] Post a thank-you story.

**Next day**
- [ ] Text or email a follow-up with the feature sheet and one question ("Anything you'd want to see again?").
- [ ] Call anyone who said they are actively buying.

**Within one week**
- [ ] Send similar listings to those who asked.
- [ ] Add visitors to your CRM with notes ([[Open House Follow-Up]], [[Lead Follow-Up Scripts]]).
- [ ] Review: turnout, leads, showings booked, seller feedback.

**Ongoing**
- Follow-up cadences from vendors: immediately, 24 hours later, one week, then monthly (Follow Up Boss); two to three touches over two weeks for warm leads (Serif). Pick a rhythm you can keep and honor opt-outs.

## 5. Common mistakes
- Working alone with no one aware of your location.
- No consent wording on the sheet.
- Over-selling or guessing about the house or neighborhood; say you will check.
- Forgetting to remove the open house sign.
- Waiting days to follow up.
