# Phone Photo Editing (Free Apps)

**In short:** Edit lightly: straighten the vertical lines, brighten the room a little, recover some window detail, and stop. Snapseed is completely free and has the tools you need. Lightroom Mobile's free tier covers basic exposure and color, but Adobe lists geometry and selective adjustments among its Premium features, so do not count on them. The line you must not cross is changing what the property actually is or looks like: removing defects, swapping skies or adding furniture without telling people.

> Verification status: Snapseed tool names come from tutorial and release-note summaries; slider values are starting points I suggest, not rules. Adobe's feature split between free and premium is described differently by different pages; confirm in the app. Disclosure rules vary by MLS and state.

## 1. Snapseed (free, iOS and Android)

Snapseed is a free Google app. The Perspective tool arrived in version 2.9 (9to5google release coverage). Tool names and positions can change after updates; some 2026 reviews complain about recent updates, so check what you see.

### A. Straighten verticals

1. Open the photo, tap **Tools** > **Perspective**.
2. Use the Vertical (and Horizontal) tilt controls so door frames and corners stand upright. You can also drag the frame corners.
3. If the tool leaves empty corners, use the fill or **Expand** options rather than cropping too hard.
4. For a simple tilt, **Rotate** has an auto-straighten option.
5. Check that the straightening did not make the room look unnatural (floors wedge-shaped, furniture stretched).

### B. Brighten the interior

1. **Tools** > **Tune Image**. Swipe up/down to choose a slider, left/right to change it.
2. Starting points (my suggestions, adjust by eye):
   - Brightness: +10 to +25
   - Ambiance: +10 to +20 (opens midtones)
   - Shadows: +20 to +40
   - Highlights: -20 to -40
   - Contrast: 0 to +10
   - Saturation: 0 to +5 (keep it low)
   - Warmth: adjust only to fix a color cast
3. **Tools** > **Selective**: tap a dark area (a hallway or corner), pinch to set the area, raise brightness slightly. Add more control points as needed.

### C. Window pull (making the view outside visible)

A "window pull" blends a darker exposure of the window into a brighter room exposure. The standard method is two or more bracketed shots blended on a computer (PhotoUp's tutorial uses Photoshop layers). I did not find a reliable free phone-only step-by-step for this.

What you can do on a phone:
1. **Best fix is at the camera:** lock exposure on the room and lower brightness a notch so windows keep detail; shoot when the sun is not on the glass.
2. **In Snapseed:** use **Selective** on the window area and lower brightness, or lower Highlights in Tune Image. Expect imperfect results on strong backlight.
3. If a window will not recover, accept a bright window rather than painting something into it.

The "range mask" workaround appears in Lightroom community threads for desktop; whether it exists in the free mobile tier is unverified.

### D. Finish

- Light **Healing** to remove dust specks or a lens smudge is generally fine. Do not remove things that are part of the property (see section 4).
- Export a copy; keep the original.

## 2. Lightroom Mobile (free tier)

Adobe says you can use all capture, organization and sharing features for free and most editing features. Its Premium features page names **geometry tools, selective adjustments, healing brush, premium presets and RAW editing** among the paid features (https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-cc/using/premium-features.html, from search summary). A comparison article lists some local adjustments as free, so the pages disagree. Check what the app offers you today.

Free-tier workflow (basic edits):
1. Crop and rotate to straighten the horizon or door frames.
2. **Light** panel: Exposure +0.2 to +0.5 if the room is dim; Highlights -30 to -50; Shadows +20 to +40.
3. **Color** panel: fix white balance with Temp/Tint. Keep Vibrance small.
4. Export as JPEG at the size your MLS asks for.

If you need perspective correction and the free tier does not offer it, use Snapseed's Perspective tool.

## 3. Editing recipe (2 minutes per photo)

- [ ] Straighten verticals (Snapseed Perspective).
- [ ] Brighten and open shadows (Tune Image).
- [ ] Pull highlights slightly for windows.
- [ ] Fix color cast.
- [ ] Zoom to 100%: check halos, banding, stretched edges.
- [ ] Compare to the original; if it looks "edited", ease off.

## 4. What is allowed vs. misleading

| Edit | Usually fine | Needs disclosure or often prohibited |
|---|---|---|
| Straighten, crop, brightness, white balance | Yes | No |
| Remove dust, lens smudge, a temporary item like a cord | Generally accepted | Check your MLS |
| Remove permanent features, defects, power lines, neighboring structures | No | Misrepresents the property |
| Sky replacement | Rules differ by MLS | MLS Now permits it only if it does not materially change views or surroundings; Bay East MLS lists sky replacement and object removal as altered images needing disclosure (earlier research draft, search-snippet level; check your own MLS) |
| Virtual staging (adding furniture) | Allowed by many MLSs with disclosure | Must be labelled; never present as the current condition; never use to hide defects |
| AI-altered images | Rules are changing quickly | California AB 723 requires disclosure and access to the unaltered originals from 1 Jan 2026 (vendor page and California Senate analysis) |

NAR's Code of Ethics Article 12 requires a "true picture" in advertising. A vendor summary says NAR staff guidance recommends clear and conspicuous disclosure for AI-altered images; this is non-binding and from vendor sources, so confirm with your association. A NAR Magazine article suggests labels should say what was changed, not just "AI used".

**Practical rule:** if a buyer could feel misled when they walk in, do not do it, or label it plainly ("Virtually staged: furniture is not included"). More: [[AI in Real Estate - Rules & Risks]], [[Listing Description Guide]].

## 5. Other free apps

- **Google Photos / iOS Photos** basic edits are enough for straightening and exposure.
- **CapCut** and **Canva** are better for social posts than for MLS photos; see [[Free Tools List]].
- Avoid heavy filters and "HDR" effects on listing photos.
