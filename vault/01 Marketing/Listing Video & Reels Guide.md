# Listing Video & Reels Guide

**In short:** Shoot one steady, well-lit walkthrough on your phone, then cut a 20-40 second vertical version for Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Move slowly, hold each shot a few seconds, lock exposure per room, and put your best shot and one key fact in the first two or three seconds. Use music that is licensed for business use (the platforms' commercial libraries) or no music at all, because popular songs in the regular libraries are often licensed for personal use only.

> Verification status: technique numbers (clip length, pan speed, pacing) are **common practice** and not rules. Platform music rules come from vendor blogs and news coverage, not from Meta's or TikTok's own policy pages, which I could not open. Re-read the current terms before posting commercial content.

## 1. Walkthrough video (horizontal or vertical master)

### Setup
- Clean lens. Phone in airplane mode or Do Not Disturb so a call does not interrupt the take.
- Lights on, blinds open, same staging checklist as [[Phone Photography Guide]].
- 4K at 30 fps is a sensible default; 1080p if storage or upload speed is limiting. 24 fps gives a more film-like look. (common practice)
- Lock exposure and focus at the start of each room (tap and hold on the room, not the window).
- Turn off iPhone "Action mode" for interiors; it crops the frame. (Action mode and its crop are described in the earlier research draft; confirm on your model.)
- Decide orientation first: one master vertical (9:16) for social, or horizontal (16:9) for the MLS and YouTube. Shooting both takes twice as long; most agents shoot vertical and a separate horizontal walkthrough only for premium listings.

### Gimbal and handheld technique
- A phone gimbal smooths walking motion ([[Gear Under $100]]). Walk heel-to-toe with soft knees; keep the gimbal around chest height. (common practice)
- No gimbal? Hold the phone with both hands, elbows tucked in, move from the hips, and use the phone's built-in stabilization. Do not stack phone stabilization on a gimbal without testing. (common practice)
- **Slow pans:** a full room sweep should take about 6-10 seconds. If you think it is too slow, it is probably right. (common practice)
- **Doorway reveals:** pause in the doorway for 2-3 seconds, then step through.
- **Direction:** move forward or sideways in one direction; avoid moving backwards.
- **Hold each shot 3-6 seconds**; the editor can trim.
- **Avoid:** fast pans, zoom, shooting into mirrors, your reflection, ceiling fans left on.

### Clip length per room (common practice)
| Space | Footage to capture |
|---|---|
| Exterior | 8-10 sec, walk-in toward the door |
| Entry | 5 sec |
| Living / kitchen | 10-15 sec each |
| Bedrooms | 5-8 sec each |
| Bathrooms | 3-5 sec each |
| Yard / view | 8-10 sec |

Final walkthrough length: about 60-90 seconds is a common target.

## 2. Vertical Reels / TikTok / Shorts structure

Working rules (common practice, supported by agent video tutorials):

1. **0-3 sec: hook.** Open on the best shot (the kitchen, the view, the pool) with one line of on-screen text: price, beds/baths, or the standout feature. No logos, no intro cards.
2. **3-20 sec: three to five quick beats.** 2-4 seconds each, cut on movement (a door opening, a pan).
3. **20-27 sec: the best feature again** or a reveal.
4. **Last 3 sec: call to action** on screen ("DM TOUR" or "Link in bio").
5. **Captions:** burn in large, high-contrast captions; many people watch without sound.
6. **Total:** 20-40 seconds for listings. YouTube Shorts can run up to 3 minutes (since October 2024, per PetaPixel as cited in an earlier research draft), but short still wins for reach. I could not confirm current Instagram Reels maximum length, so it is not stated here.
7. **Keep the key subject out of the bottom ~20% and the right edge** where app buttons sit (common practice).

Repeat one master edit across platforms, with platform-appropriate captions ([[Social Media Playbook]]).

### Editing
- **CapCut** is a free mobile and desktop editor with auto-captions and templates. The free plan is limited: sources disagree on caption caps and export resolution, and some templates and effects need Pro. CapCut's own help page explains Standard vs Pro: https://www.capcut.com/help/difference-between-standard-and-pro-subscription . See [[Free Tools List]].
- Instagram's and TikTok's built-in editors are enough for trims, text and sound.
- Add a title or lower-third only if it does not cover the property.
- Check your MLS and broker rules for required disclosures (brokerage name, license number) in video and captions.

## 3. Music licensing for business accounts

**Why it matters:** music in the general libraries is licensed for personal use. Using it for business promotion can get posts muted or removed, or lead to copyright claims.

### Instagram / Meta
- Business accounts reportedly get a smaller set of commercially cleared tracks than personal or creator accounts (a vendor blog puts it at roughly 14,000 tracks; the count is **unverified**). Sources disagree on whether switching to a Creator account restores the full library, and it may vary by region.
- One source says Meta's licensed library is intended for personal, non-commercial use and that library tracks may not be licensed for paid promotion (boosted posts/ads), so a boosted Reel could be rejected or pulled. Library licenses apply to Instagram only, not other platforms.
- Safe options: original audio (your own voiceover), tracks from the music library available to your account type, or music you have licensed yourself and add before uploading.
- Source quality: vendor blogs, no Meta policy page. Check Meta's current music guidelines.

### TikTok
- TikTok keeps two libraries: the general sound library for personal accounts and the **Commercial Music Library (CML)** for business accounts. Business accounts see the CML in the in-app editor by default. Music you license yourself, added to the video before upload, is also allowed (Social Media Today coverage and vendor blogs; TikTok's own help page not opened).
- No source I found says whether a real estate agent counts as a business account, but promoting listings is marketing, so use the Commercial Music Library or no music.
- TikTok's audio and trending sounds from personal accounts are the risky ones for business posts.

### What I suggest
- Prefer original audio or a clearly licensed library track.
- Do not download a popular song and add it in a separate app.
- If you cross-post the same file to YouTube, Instagram and TikTok, use music you licensed for all platforms, because platform-library licenses are platform-specific.
- Keep receipts or screenshots of the license for any music you buy.

## 4. Shot list template (30-40 second Reel)

- [ ] **Hook (0-3s):** best interior shot or front door open; text "3 bed | 2 bath | $___"
- [ ] **Exterior reveal (3-6s):** slow push-in toward the entry
- [ ] **Entry / living (6-10s)**
- [ ] **Kitchen detail (10-14s):** counters, appliances, island
- [ ] **Primary bedroom (14-18s)**
- [ ] **Bath detail (18-21s)**
- [ ] **Outdoor or view (21-27s)**
- [ ] **Best feature repeat (27-30s)**
- [ ] **Call to action (30-33s):** "Open house Sat 1-3" or "DM TOUR". (Open house details are fine on social, but not in MLS public remarks.)
- [ ] Captions on; music from a commercial library or none; brokerage name in caption or end card per your state/broker rules.

## 5. Shoot-day checklist

- [ ] Battery full; storage free; lens cleaned
- [ ] Do Not Disturb on
- [ ] Lights on, blinds open, clutter gone
- [ ] Gimbal balanced and tested outside the property
- [ ] Shot list printed or on screen
- [ ] Exposure locked per room
- [ ] Review each clip on the phone before leaving
