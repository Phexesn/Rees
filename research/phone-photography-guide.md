## Phone capabilities

General rules that apply to every phone in this section:

- **Interiors:** use the 0.5x ultra-wide lens (the "0.5x" or "ultra-wide" option) when the room is small. Use 1x for rooms where the 0.5x view shows strong fisheye bowing at the edges. (common practice)
- **HDR:** all current phones merge multiple exposures automatically (Apple Smart HDR / Deep Fusion, Samsung HDR, Google HDR+). For real estate, this is usually helpful, but it can cause halos around windows and ceiling lines. See "Common mistakes".
- **Locking focus and exposure:** tap the screen on the target and hold until "AE/AF Lock" appears (iPhone) or the equivalent lock indicator appears (Android). This keeps the focus and exposure fixed while you recompose. (common practice; exact label varies by OS version, verify)
- **Exposure compensation:** on iPhone, tap the screen and drag the sun icon up or down. On Samsung and Pixel, use the +/- exposure control in the default camera app. (common practice; verify for your model)
- **Gridlines:** iPhone: Settings > Camera > Grid. Samsung: camera settings menu, then "Picture guidelines" or "Grid lines" (verify on your One UI version). Pixel: camera settings, then "Grid type" (verify).
- **Level indicator:** iPhone 13 and later show a level in the camera app when the phone is held level; the path to enable it is verify. Samsung and Pixel offer a level or horizon guide in the same settings area as gridlines. (verify)
- **RAW:** capture is available on all phones below, but the menu path and the RAW format differ by brand. Use RAW only if you will edit in a RAW-capable app. For most agents, a standard HEIC/JPEG output with good exposure is enough.

## Settings cheat sheets

All values below are starting points. Test one room first, review on a large screen, and adjust. Values are (common practice) unless marked otherwise.

### Interiors (all phones)

| Setting | Starting value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lens | 0.5x ultra-wide | 1x for tight rooms or to reduce fisheye bowing |
| Exposure compensation | −0.3 to −0.7 EV | Window-heavy rooms: −0.7 to −1.0 |
| ISO (Pro mode) | Ceiling 100–400 | Above 400 noise becomes visible on screen |
| Shutter speed (handheld) | Auto; avoid below 1/60 s | Blurring starts below about 1/60 s |
| Shutter speed (tripod) | 1/30 s to 1/8 s | Use a 2-second timer or remote to avoid shake |
| White balance (mixed light) | 5000–5600K | Match all lights to the same color first |
| Focus | Lock on a mid-depth point, such as a counter edge | Use AE/AF lock |
| Flash | Off | Flash creates harsh shadows and hot spots |
| Grid | On | Use the grid to keep verticals straight |
| Resolution | Highest standard resolution (12MP or 48MP, depending on phone) | Use a 1:1 or 4:3 frame only if the MLS requires it |

### Exteriors

| Setting | Starting value |
|---|---|
| Lens | 1x (main) for the front facade; 0.5x only if the house is close to a wall |
| Exposure compensation | 0 to −0.3 |
| Time | Sun behind photographer; see "Time-of-day shot calendar" |
| Focus | Lock on the front door or main facade |
| Grid | On; keep the house edges parallel with the grid |

### Twilight

| Setting | Starting value |
|---|---|
| Time | 15–30 minutes after sunset, while the sky still holds blue (common practice) |
| Tripod | Required; shutter 1/4 s to 2 s, depending on light |
| ISO (Pro mode) | 100–200 |
| White balance | 4500–5500K, or auto if the sky looks neutral |
| Lights | Turn on all interior and exterior lights before shooting |

### Video (walkthrough)

| Setting | Starting value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution | 4K | Use 1080p only if storage or upload speed is limiting |
| Frame rate | 30fps for standard walkthroughs; 24fps for a more cinematic feel | 24fps gives more motion blur, which can look smoother on slow pans |
| Exposure | Lock AE/AF at the start of each room | Re-lock if the light changes |
| Stabilization | Standard stabilization for handheld; gimbal stabilization if a gimbal is attached | Do not stack phone stabilization on top of a gimbal unless testing shows it helps; this is a common source of jitter (common practice; test) |
| Action mode (iPhone 14+) | Off for interiors | Crops the frame |
| Log / ProRes | Not needed for listing video | Adds editing time |

## Free editing apps

Free apps for a typical interior. Starting values are common practice; the exact labels may differ by app version (verify in the app).

### Snapseed (free, iOS and Android)

Snapseed is completely free and includes straightening and perspective tools. [src: Digital Trends, https://www.digitaltrends.com/photography/snapseed-perspective-tool/]

1. Open the photo. Tap **Tools > Perspective** (Geometry). Use **Auto** first, then adjust **Vertical** and **Horizontal** by hand so that walls and door frames are plumb. (verify label)
2. Tap **Tools > Crop**. Keep the composition level. Crop out stray edges, and leave a little headroom.
3. Tap **Tools > Tune Image** (or **Tune**). Starting values for a typical bright interior:
   - Ambience: +10 to +20 (adds contrast in the midtones)
   - Highlights: −40 (recovers window detail)
   - Shadows: +30 (opens dark corners)
   - Contrast: +5 to +10
   - Saturation: 0 to +5 (do not go higher)
4. Tap **Tools > Selective** to brighten a specific dark area (for example, a hallway) by +10 to +20 exposure, without touching the windows. (common practice)
5. Tap **Tools > Healing** to remove small distractions such as cords and outlets. Do not remove anything that changes the room's features. (common practice)

### Lightroom Mobile (free tier)

Adobe's free version includes basic editing tools, presets, and crop; Premium adds features such as Generative Remove and Lens Blur. [src: Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop-lightroom/mobile; PictureCorrect, https://www.picturecorrect.com/exploring-lightroom-mobile-free-features-vs-premium/] Confirm current tier contents in the app.

1. Open the photo. Tap **Edit**, then **Geometry** (or **Crop**). Use **Upright** or **Vertical** to straighten the walls. Confirm availability in the free tier (verify).
2. In **Light**, starting values: Exposure −0.2 to +0.2; Highlights −40; Shadows +30; Whites +10 to +20; Blacks −5 to −10.
3. In **Color**, starting values: Temperature (Warmth) +0 to +300 for a neutral look; Tint 0; Vibrance +5 to +10; Saturation 0.
4. In **Detail**, Sharpening 25–40, Noise Reduction 0–15. Higher noise reduction softens fine detail, so keep it low for listing photos. (common practice)
5. Save as a copy; never overwrite the original.

### VSCO

VSCO's free app is best for stylized social media, not for primary listing photos. The full filter set is behind a paid subscription. [src: search snippet, not verified: the subscription price was not confirmed from a primary page; verify before quoting a figure.]

### What not to do

- **Oversaturation.** Keep Saturation at 0 to +5 and Vibrance at +5 to +10. Heavy saturation makes wood, walls and grass look unnatural and erodes trust when buyers visit in person. (common practice)
- **Crushing shadows or blowing highlights.** Shadows above about +40 can look muddy. If window highlights are clipped (white with no detail), return to the exposure step and reshoot. (common practice)
- **Fake skies, object removal, or virtual staging without disclosure.** MLS rules vary. Many MLSs require disclosure of virtual staging and altered images. One MLS (MLS Now) allows sky replacement only if it does not materially change the views or surroundings. Another (Bay East MLS) lists sky replacement and object removal as altered images that need disclosure. [src: MLS Now Rules Section 3, https://support.mlsnow.com/hc/en-us/articles/47635315367067-MLS-Now-Rules-Section-3-Images-Media-Virtual-Staging; Bay East, https://bayeast.org/?p=169314] Sources disagree on sky replacement, so check your local MLS rulebook before editing any sky or staging element. NAR's 2023 Field Guide reportedly says to disclose virtual staging, never use it to hide defects, and never present it as the current state of the property (secondary summary; NAR page not fetched).
- **Removing or adding structural features** (windows, walls, fixtures) or hiding defects such as water stains or cracks. This misrepresents the property. (common practice)
- **Stacking filters.** Use one preset at most; check the final image on a second device or at a smaller size.

## Lighting by room orientation

Natural light depends on compass direction. Rules below are (common practice) unless noted. Sun times vary with latitude and season (see the calendar).

| Room facing | Best time for natural light | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| East | Morning (roughly 7–10 a.m.) | Warm, low, direct light. Strong contrast; may need −0.7 EV |
| South | Midday to early afternoon | Strongest and most even light in the northern hemisphere; may cause harsh highlights |
| West | Afternoon (roughly 3–6 p.m.) | Warm light; can be harsh in summer |
| North | Even, soft light all day | No direct sun; shadows are soft. Best for detail and color accuracy; may look dim, so use interior lights |

- **Exterior, front facade:** photograph when the sun is behind the photographer and lighting the facade. For a front facing south, this is roughly late morning to early afternoon. For east-facing, morning. For west-facing, late afternoon. For north-facing, use an overcast day or early morning or late evening in summer. (common practice)
- **Twilight:** 15–30 minutes after sunset, while the sky still holds blue, and interior lights are on. (common practice)
- **Seasonal:** summer days are long, and the sun is high at midday, so exteriors are easier at 10 a.m.–2 p.m. Winter sun is low; the afternoon can give warm light on west-facing facades, but the usable window is short. (common practice)
- **Turn on all lights,** including lamps, ceiling fixtures and under-cabinet lights. Match all bulbs to one color temperature (ideally 3000–4000K). Turn off mixed sources that you cannot match (for example, a single cool LED in a warm room). (common practice)
- **Curtains and blinds:** open them fully for the photo, unless the view is poor. Tilt blinds to diffuse direct sun. Keep the view clear of neighboring structures when possible. (common practice)

## Composition rules

- **Camera height:** chest height, roughly 4 to 5 feet (about 1.2–1.5 m). Lower positions make ceilings look short; higher positions make rooms look bigger and show more floor. (common practice)
- **Shoot from corners or doorways.** A corner shot shows two walls and more of the room; a doorway shot shows the room layout. (common practice)
- **Keep verticals straight.** Hold the phone level. Use the grid. Tilting the phone upward causes converging vertical lines, which looks like the room is falling over. (common practice)
- **One-point vs two-point perspective:** one-point (the camera faces a wall straight on) suits hallways and symmetric rooms. Two-point (the camera is angled) shows more of the room and suits most interiors. Use two-point with the phone level. (common practice)
- **Horizontal vs vertical:** horizontal frames are the default for MLS and portals. Use vertical only for hallways, stair details, or social posts (see the Reels section). MLS rules on orientation vary; check your board. (common practice)
- **Photo order and count:** each MLS sets its own limits. Sourced examples:
  - GBRAR (Georgia): minimum of 5 photos for non-new-construction residential. [src: https://gbrar.com/?p=2476]
  - Doorify: minimum of 3 photos for residential, 1 for new construction, uploaded within 7 days of going live; maximum 100. [src: https://support.doorifymls.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058227993-What-are-the-Photo-Requirements-for-a-listing]
  - Unlock MLS: maximum 40 photos; a front-of-property shot is required. [src: https://support.unlockmls.com/hc/en-us/articles/48693110642963-How-to-Input-and-Update-Photos-in-Unlock-MLS]
  - MLS Now: maximum 50 photos. [src: https://support.mlsnow.com/hc/en-us/articles/24915189546907-Listing-Photo-Rules-and-Requirements]
  - Perchwell: at least 1 photo at entry. [src: https://intercom.help/perchwell-help-center-baldwin/en/articles/14710094-top-compliance-questions]
  - Vendor marketing claims that brokerages recommend 20–25 photos are not rule text and should be treated with caution. [src: search snippet only]
- **Shot order for a typical listing (common practice):** front exterior; front entry; living room (two angles); kitchen (two angles); dining; primary bedroom; primary bathroom; other bedrooms and bathrooms; backyard or pool; key features (fireplace, view). Lead with the strongest photo.
- **Minimum pixel size:** 1024×768 is widely cited by vendor blogs; check your MLS. (verify)

## Staging checklist

Room-by-room declutter checklist (common practice):

**All rooms**
- Remove personal photos, diplomas, religious items, and mail.
- Turn off or remove visible cables, wires and power strips.
- Remove trash cans, cleaning supplies, pet bowls and toys from view.
- Turn off ceiling fans if their blades show; keep lights on.
- Make beds, fold throws, straighten pillows.

**Kitchen**
- Clear counters except one or two items (a plant, a bowl of fruit, a coffee maker).
- Remove magnets and notes from the fridge.
- Wipe appliances and stovetop; close cabinet doors.

**Bathroom**
- Lower toilet seats; close the lid.
- Remove toiletries from the tub and counter, or put them in a drawer.
- Hang clean towels; remove bath mats that look stained.
- Clean mirrors. Keep the phone and photographer out of mirrors (see "Common mistakes").

**Bedroom**
- Remove laundry and clothing from chairs and floors.
- Make the bed with the best linens.
- Close closet doors or tidy them.

**Living room**
- Remove remotes, cables, throw pillows that are out of place.
- Fluff sofa cushions; open blinds or curtains.

**Exterior and driveway**
- Remove cars from the driveway and front of the house; park them on the street if they block the view.
- Mow the lawn; trim edges; remove leaves or debris.
- Put away bins, hoses, toys and outdoor furniture covers.
- Turn on exterior lights for twilight shots.

**Backyard and pool**
- Clean the pool and skim it; remove pool toys and equipment that show.
- Put away grills, tools and trash bins.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Leaning verticals.** Cause: tilting the phone up or down. Fix: hold the phone level; use the grid; straighten in Snapseed or Lightroom Geometry. (common practice)
- **Blown-out windows.** Cause: the phone exposes for the room, and the window goes white. Fixes:
  - Use exposure compensation of −0.7 to −1.0 EV for window-heavy rooms, or tap the room interior (not the window) and lock.
  - Shoot when the outside light is lower (late afternoon or overcast) so the window contrast is smaller.
  - In editing, pull Highlights down (−40 to −60) to recover window detail.
  (common practice)
- **HDR haloing.** Cause: HDR merges leave bright halos along edges near windows or ceilings. Fix: reduce HDR contrast in editing; avoid heavy Clarity or Structure sliders; try a shot with HDR turned off if the phone allows it (verify menu). (common practice)
- **Mixed color temperatures.** Cause: daylight from windows mixed with warm bulbs. Fixes: turn on all lights and match them; set white balance to 5000–5600K in Pro mode; in editing, use Warmth or Temperature sliders. (common practice)
- **Mirror reflections (photographer in the mirror).** Fix: step to the side of the mirror; shoot from a lower or higher angle; put a cloth over the mirror edge; or shoot from a doorway. Use the grid to check edges. (common practice)
- **Wide-angle distortion at edges.** Cause: the 0.5x ultra-wide stretches objects near the frame edge. Fix: keep key features near the center; shoot at 1x for tight rooms; use Perspective in Snapseed to correct edge bowing. (common practice)
- **Blurry low-light shots.** Cause: slow shutter speed, shaking, or Night Sight with movement. Fixes: use a tripod with a 2-second timer; add light (turn on all lamps); avoid Night Sight for listing photos unless the room is very dim; shoot at ISO 100–400. (common practice)

  - For homes priced between $200,000 and $1 million, DSLR-photographed homes sold for $3,400 to $11,200 more relative to list price.

## Video walkthrough technique

- **Slow pans:** pan at roughly one room-width in 5–8 seconds. A 180° sweep should take about 8–12 seconds. Move the phone at the same speed throughout. (common practice)
- **Doorway transitions:** stand in the doorway, hold for 2–3 seconds, then walk through. This makes a clean cut between rooms. (common practice)
- **Exterior-to-interior sequence:** start on the street or driveway (3–5 seconds), walk to the front door, enter, and show the entry hallway. (common practice)
- **Gimbal walk cadence ("ninja walk"):** walk with bent knees and heel-to-toe steps, keeping your upper body still. Keep the gimbal at chest height. Walk at a constant pace. (common practice)
- **Clip length per room:** 8–15 seconds for small rooms; 15–25 seconds for living and kitchen areas. (common practice)
- **Total target length:** 60–90 seconds for a full walkthrough; under 60 seconds for social clips. (common practice)
- **Video settings:** 4K at 30fps; lock exposure per room; keep a steady walking speed. (common practice; see the video settings table)

## Vertical Reels/TikTok capture

- **Hook (first 2 seconds):** start with the best feature (a kitchen island, a view, a pool). Use a text overlay with the key fact ("3 bed / 2 bath / $X"). Avoid starting with a blank wall or a closed door. (common practice)
- **Pacing:** cut every 1–3 seconds for fast-paced clips; slower (3–5 seconds) for a calm walkthrough. (common practice)
- **Shot list template (30–45 seconds):**
  1. Hook: best feature (2 s)
  2. Exterior reveal (3 s)

## Time-of-day shot calendar

Continental US. Sun times vary by latitude and season. Use these as starting points, and check the actual sun position in a sun-path app before shooting. (common practice)

Front facing direction is the direction the front of the house faces. The backyard faces the opposite direction.

| Front faces | Best time for front exterior | Backyard faces | Best time for backyard | Pool shots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North | Overcast day; early morning or late evening in summer when the sun swings north (sun is mostly behind the house) | South | Late morning to early afternoon (sun over the yard) | Late morning to early afternoon, when the sun is high over the pool |
| South | Late morning to early afternoon (sun high, in the south) | North | Overcast day or early morning/late evening in summer | Late morning to early afternoon if the pool is on the south side; avoid harsh glare on water |
| East | Morning, roughly 7–10 a.m. | West | Afternoon, roughly 3–6 p.m. | Afternoon for a west-facing pool |
| West | Afternoon, roughly 3–6 p.m. | East | Morning, roughly 7–10 a.m. | Morning for an east-facing pool |

Seasonal adjustments:

- **Summer:** sun is high and rises in the northeast; sunsets are late. Front and pool shots are easier between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m. Harsh midday light can cause strong shadows; use exposure compensation. (common practice)
- **Winter:** sun is low and rises in the southeast; sunsets are early. Afternoon light is warmer and longer; a window of 1–2 hours is typical for most exterior sides. Plan shoots for late morning to early afternoon. (common practice)
- **Spring and fall:** the sun is in between; the schedule for summer or winter works as a midpoint. (common practice)

## Quick plan (20 minutes)

For a standard listing with a small or medium home and existing furniture:

1. **Minutes 0–3: prepare.** Turn on all lights. Open blinds and curtains. Remove cars from the driveway. Lower toilet seats. Clear kitchen counters.
2. **Minutes 3–5: exterior.** Stand at the front door-side angle that puts the sun behind you. Use 1x lens, level phone, grid on. Take 3–5 shots.
3. **Minutes 5–12: interiors.** For each room (living, kitchen, primary bedroom, bathroom, one or two more):
   - Set 0.5x (or 1x for tight rooms).
   - Set exposure −0.5 EV for window rooms.
   - Shoot from a corner or doorway at chest height, 2–3 frames per room, and pick the best.
4. **Minutes 12–15: video (optional).** Record one 60-second walkthrough at 4K 30fps, with slow pans and doorway transitions.
5. **Minutes 15–20: edit.** In Snapseed, straighten verticals with Perspective (Auto, then adjust); set Highlights −40, Shadows +30, Contrast +5; do not oversaturate. Export at the MLS size and check the MLS count.

## Weekend plan (premium)

For a larger or luxury home, or a listing where you want a stronger result (not a professional shoot, but more time):

**Friday evening: scout and plan**
- Check sun position for the front and backyard (sun-path app). Pick the time for each shot.
- Create a room-by-room shot list with two angles per room.
- Plan the staging: remove personal items, plan a fresh arrangement for key rooms.

**Saturday morning: exteriors**
- 8–10 a.m. (east-facing) or 10 a.m.–2 p.m. (south-facing): front, driveway, street view. Use a tripod for stability.
- Shoot 8–12 frames; pick the best 3–5.

**Saturday late morning to afternoon: interiors**
- Bring a 1x and 0.5x lens setup. Use the tripod in darker rooms.
- Turn on all lights; match to 3000–4000K.
- Use AE/AF lock per room. Bracket exposures (−1, 0, +1 EV) in Pro mode for the windows; blend in editing if your app supports it. (common practice; check app)

**Saturday evening: twilight**
- 15–30 minutes after sunset: front exterior with all lights on. Tripod, 1/4–2 s, ISO 100–200.

**Sunday morning: video and social content**
- One 90-second 4K walkthrough, with a gimbal (for example, DJI Osmo Mobile SE).
- One vertical 30–45 second Reel with the shot list above.

**Sunday afternoon: edit and review**
- Edit in Snapseed or Lightroom Mobile (free tier). Straighten, adjust highlights and shadows, and check every photo at full size.
- Check each photo against your MLS rules (count, size, disclosure). Disclose any altered image or virtual staging.
